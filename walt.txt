In Arkansas, Sebastian, Crawford, Scott, Polk, Washington, Boone, Benton, Madison, Carroll, Yell, Pope, Johnson, Newton, Franklin, and Logan Counties. Far Eastern Oklahoma and Western Arkansas: Monday through Sunday, Daytime highs in the 60s to 80s. Low temperatures from 40s to 60s. Winds calm to 25 miles per hour at times. Thunderstorms are possible on Friday Night and Saturday. WALT Model AI for Friday Night supports wind gusts could reach 40 to 60 mph, which is enough to blow around loose objects, break small tree limbs, and possibly cause isolated minor damage. Hail is possible, mainly in the pea to quarter-size range, with a few spots maybe a little larger in the strongest part of the storm. You could also get heavy rain, lightning, and loud thunder as the storm moves through. Because the shear is 2, there is enough rotation in the environment for a brief funnel cloud or weak tornado to be possible, but that would not be the main threat. Overall, this looks like a low-end severe setup with gusty winds, some hail, and a small tornado risk. WALT AI Saturday for Western Arkansas and far Southeast Oklahoma (near Idabel) supports winds may gust between 60 and 80 mph, which is enough to knock down tree limbs/trees, blow around loose objects, and possibly cause some roof, and barn damage. There’s also a good chance of large hail, potentially around the size of quarters or even golf balls. On top of that, the atmosphere has just enough spin in it that a tornado could develop, especially if a storm strengthens in the right spot. It doesn’t guarantee a tornado will hit, but it’s definitely something to keep an eye on.
