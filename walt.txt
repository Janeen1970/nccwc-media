Far Eastern Oklahoma and Western Arkansas: Friday through Thursday, Daytime highs in the 60s to 80s. Low temperatures from 30s to 60s. Winds calm to 25 miles per hour at times. Thunderstorms possible on Friday into Saturday morning. WALT AI on Friday evening supports a very intense round of storms moving through western portions of Eastern Oklahoma and Northwest Arkansas, with the biggest concern being damaging winds that could reach 60 to 80 mph plus. That’s strong enough to knock down trees, tear off parts of roofs, structurally damage barns, and cause power outages. You’ll likely see a fast-moving line of storms that can hit suddenly, bringing heavy rain, loud thunder, damaging winds, and bursts of large hail that could dent cars and break windows. There’s also a chance for brief tornadoes to spin up quickly. Overall, this is the kind of storm system where things can go from calm to severe very quickly. WALT Model AI Saturday morning for the rest of Eastern Oklahoma and Western Arkansas supports 20 to 40 mph winds in storms, but areas of wind damage, 60 to 80 mph, are possible in stronger storms. Hail from pea size to golfball size is possible. There’s also a chance for brief tornadoes to spin up quickly, but the potential is more isolated compared to Friday evening. WALT Model AI for Thursday and Friday of next week suggests severe thunderstorms are expected to move through the area, bringing damaging wind gusts between 60 and 80 mph that could knock down trees, damage roofs, and cause power outages. Large hail, possibly around golf ball size, which can dent vehicles and break windows, may develop as well. The storms could become intense quickly with heavy rain, loud thunder, damaging winds, large hail, and frequent lightning. There is also a risk of a brief tornado or funnel cloud developing, especially in the stronger parts of the storm. Overall, expect a dangerous round of storms with wind, hail, and isolated tornado potential.
