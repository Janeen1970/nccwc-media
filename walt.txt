Far Eastern Oklahoma and Western Arkansas: Sunday through Saturday, Daytime highs in the 60s to 80s. Low temperatures from 30s to 60s. Winds calm to 25 miles per hour at times. Thunderstorms are possible on Sunday through Wednesday morning and on Thursday. WALT Model AI on Sunday supports 40–60 mph wind gusts, strong enough to break small limbs, blow around loose objects, and cause isolated power issues. Hail is possible, mainly around pea to quarter size, with a few stronger cores possibly producing slightly larger hail. With shear high, some rotation is possible, so a brief weak tornado or funnel cloud cannot be ruled out, but the main threats are gusty winds, hail, lightning, and heavy rain. WALT Model AI for Tuesday supports a storm environment that is mainly wind and hail threat. Wind could reach 60 to 80 mph or more, strong enough to knock down trees, damage roofs, and cause power outages. Hail could reach quarter to baseball size, maybe large apple size in the strongest storm core. With shear still moderate, tornado risk is not the main threat, but a brief EF-0 to EF-1 spin-up or wall cloud stage is possible. WALT Model AI for Thursday into Friday morning supports a line of storms with moderate winds and some hail, but nothing extreme. The winds could gust up to around 40 to 60 mph, which may cause minor damage, and hail could reach about quarter size. There is a better chance of seeing brief gustnadoes along the leading edge of the storm where winds collide, but the overall environment does not support well-developed tornadoes. In simple terms, this is more of a wind-driven storm with a small chance of whirlwinds rather than a true tornado-producing system. WALT Model AI is an innovative forecasting system developed by climate scientist Wayne Adams. The technology is currently copyrighted and filed for patent protection in 2025. It represents a new approach to environmental hazard prediction. The system is capable of producing forecasts at multiple scales, including area-wide, city-level, and street-level analysis. During adverse weather events, the model can provide detailed city-level forecasts to help identify potential impact zones. Future upgrades will include optional house-level forecasting services available through a premium feature.
