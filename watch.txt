   .PREV DISCUSSION... /ISSUED 1135 AM CDT Sat Aug 15 2026/

   ...Front Range/Central High Plains...
   Modestly strong southwesterly flow aloft coupled with strong
   daytime heating, low-level upslope flow, and orographic lift will
   support another day of thunderstorms regionally. These thunderstorms
   should gradually move off the higher terrain into the central High
   Plains later this afternoon, where a moderately
   buoyant airmass will be in place. Around 30-35 kt of effective
   bulk shear is expected over the region, which will support a mixed
   mode with supercells and multicell line segments. Isolated large
   hail is possible with any supercells, while severe wind gusts will
   be possible with both supercells and line segments. The severe
   threat should persist into the evening as this complex of storms
   moves eastward through the central High Plains including
   west/southwest Kansas.

   ...Midwest/Middle Ohio Valley...
   Thunderstorms have generally weakened since the overnight, although
   scattered showers/some thunderstorms and plentiful cloud cover
   continue in areas progressively north of the Ohio River. An MCV is
   readily apparent in observational data as it continues to move into
   northwest Indiana, with an enhanced belt (40+ kt) of westerly winds
   associated with the MCV per regional WSR-88D VWP data. 

   As clouds thin/abate, a gradual increase in renewed thunderstorm
   coverage and intensity is expected later this afternoon. Mid-level
   lapse rates will be poor, but dewpoints in the 70s F will support
   moderate buoyancy, particularly in areas where cloud cover does not
   overly inhibit diurnal heating, which will tend to generally be
   southward toward the Ohio River. Strong updrafts and high
   precipitable water will support the potential for wet microbursts
   capable of producing damaging gusts, and some enhancement to the
   low-level winds could also yield a brief tornado.

   ...Central Appalachians/Carolinas/Georgia...
   A very moist airmass, characterized by dewpoints in the low to mid
   70s, is in place regionally. Strong heating will destabilize the
   airmass by the early afternoon, resulting in a large reservoir of
   moderate buoyancy. Thunderstorm development is anticipated within
   this destabilized airmass across the higher terrain of the central
   Appalachians as well as any area of low-level convergence across the
   remainder of the region. Vertical shear will be weak, and limited
   storm organization is anticipated. Even so, the moderate buoyancy
   could still support updrafts/downdrafts strong enough to produce
   occasional damaging gusts.

   ...Central Plains/Mid Missouri Valley to Mid Mississippi Valley...
   Elevated thunderstorms are expected again tonight, initiated by both
   an approaching cold front and warm-air advection. Ample low-level
   moisture and moderate buoyancy will be in place, but vertical shear
   will be marginal. Isolated strong to severe storms are expected, but
   a predominantly elevated and clustered storm mode should limit the
   overall severe potential.
