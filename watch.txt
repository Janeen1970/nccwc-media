   ...SUMMARY...
   Severe thunderstorms with damaging wind gusts will be possible today
   from the eastern Iowa/northern Illinois vicinity into parts of the
   Ohio Valley and central Appalachians.  Areas of severe wind gusts
   and hail will also be possible over parts of the northern Plains.

   ...IA to the Mid-Atlantic and Southeast...
   An uncertain and inherently complex forecast is apparent this
   morning from IA east-southeastward into the central Appalachians. 
   An impulse embedded within west-northwest flow and an overnight MCV
   moving into IA from NE appear to be aiding in MCS development this
   morning over eastern IA, with this thunderstorm complex moving into
   northern IL through the late morning.  

   While the exact evolution of a potential severe MCS moving across
   the Midwest is uncertain, a possible scenario is the thunderstorm
   activity over IA either merges with additional storms developing
   ahead of it or it continues to intensify into the main severe-wind
   producing MCS.  Another severe cluster is located over eastern IN
   within the eastern envelope of the EML.  Considered higher wind
   probabilities to be delineated this outlook update, but it remains
   unclear regarding sequential details that would lead to a
   high-confidence mesoscale corridor of severe wind and associated
   wind damage.  Nonetheless, environmental parameters (e.g., very rich
   moisture, steep 700-500 mb lapse rates, adequate deep-layer shear)
   will strongly favor the development of mature/severe linear
   clusters/bows with the more intense storms that eventually evolve
   today.  This activity will likely move along a large CAPE gradient
   that will likely be reinforced by early morning convection. 
   Scattered severe gusts (60-85 mph) will be likely in the favored
   mesoscale corridors with swaths of concentrated wind damage.  A
   lower but appreciable severe risk may extend into VA and NC with a
   lower severe threat with south extent into SC/GA.  

   ...Northern Plains...
   East/southeasterly flow along the surface trough is forecast to
   maintain 60s F dewpoints across the western Dakotas and parts of NE
   this afternoon.  Steep midlevel lapse rates atop the relatively
   moist boundary layer, and strong heating will support a corridor of
   moderate to strong instability across the western Dakotas. 
   Scattered storms are expected by early evening amid supercell wind
   profiles.  Elongated hodographs favorable for splitting cells with
   an accompanying risk for large to very large hail are forecast. 
   Isolated severe gusts also could occur.  Given the lack of a
   stronger low-level jet during the nighttime hours, it appears
   unlikely that upscale growth will occur, and convection is expected
   to wane with eastward extent overnight.

   ...Mojave Desert of CA...
   Early morning radar imagery shows an MCV moving slowly northwest
   over the lower CO River Valley and this feature is likely associated
   with a weak disturbance moving northwest into southern CA.  The
   airmass across the desert will undergo strong heating in wake of
   early day cloud debris.  Another likely convectively augmented
   mid-level impulse will move across southern AZ towards southern CA
   today.  Widely scattered thunderstorms are forecast to develop this
   afternoon amid a seasonably moist airmass and an inverted-V
   thermodynamic profile is depicted in forecast soundings.  Isolated
   severe gusts are possible with the more intense downdrafts.
