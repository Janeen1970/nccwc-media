   Day 1 Convective Outlook

   1254 AM CDT Mon Aug 03 2026

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS LATE THIS
   AFTERNOON AND EVENING ACROSS PARTS OF NORTHERN MINNESOTA INTO
   ADJACENT PORTIONS OF THE EASTERN DAKOTAS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Strong to severe thunderstorm development appears possible today
   across parts of northern Minnesota into adjacent portions of the
   eastern Dakotas, as well as across the eastern Arizona international
   border vicinity and parts of the northern Mid Atlantic through
   western New England.

   ...Discussion...
   A compact, but fairly deep, lower/mid-tropospheric cyclone over the
   Canadian Prairies is forecast to migrate across northern Manitoba
   into western Hudson Bay today through tonight.  One initial trailing
   cold front is already advancing into the northern Great Plains, with
   a more substantive cold front approaching the international border
   and forecast to overtake it by mid afternoon, roughly across the
   northwestern Minnesota through southeastern North Dakota, central
   South Dakota, and western Nebraska vicinity.  

   As a notable upstream mid-level short wave trough accelerates across
   the northern U.S. Rockies, toward northwestern Ontario, it appears
   that a secondary surface low emerging from Wyoming will weaken while
   migrating northeast of the Black Hills through the adjacent middle
   Missouri Valley early today.  Another surface low may form along the
   front across northern Minnesota into northwestern Ontario this
   evening, but it appears that the most rapid deepening will occur
   north of Lake Superior overnight.

   To the south and southwest of the mid-level wave, models indicate
   that mid-level ridging will build across the Southwest during the
   day, with highest heights becoming centered along an axis across
   southwestern through central Arizona into north central New Mexico
   by late afternoon.

   Within the downstream westerlies, a notable mid-level trough is
   forecast to pivot east and northeast of the lower Great Lakes
   through New England.  As it does, a remnant perturbation digging
   toward the southern Appalachians may evolve into a weak mid-level
   low across the Southeast, cut off from the westerlies.

   ...Northern Great Plains into Upper Midwest...
   The influence of possible early period convection and associated
   cloud cover on destabilization and convective potential later today
   across parts of the central and eastern Dakotas into Minnesota
   remains a bit unclear.  However, by mid to late afternoon, guidance
   generally indicates that a fairly sharp quasi-stationary
   near-surface baroclinic zone will evolve across northwestern
   Minnesota through southeastern North Dakota.  It appears that this
   may become a focus for at least a narrow corridor of modest
   boundary-layer destabilization, coincident with strengthening south
   to southwesterly lower/mid-tropospheric wind fields (including
   30-40+ kt around 850 mb and 50-70 kt around 500 mb).  This may
   provide a window of opportunity for strong to severe storm
   development, including a couple of supercells, by this evening,
   before the front overtakes the instability axis with the passage of
   the frontal wave.

   ...Southeastern Arizona/adjacent New Mexico...
   Low-level moisture appears likely to remain relatively modest across
   central and southern Arizona, with strong daytime heating and mixing
   lowering surface dew points through the mid to lower 50s F by late
   afternoon.  However, along the southern periphery of the stronger
   mid-level ridging, it appears that this may support at least
   moderate boundary layer CAPE near the international border vicinity,
   beneath 25+ kt east-northeasterly mid-level steering flow. 

   The higher terrain either side of the southern Arizona/New Mexico
   state border will provide potential for a more substantive
   clustering of convection, which may propagate across and south of
   the international border by early this evening.  If preceding
   thunderstorm development, initially developing in the unstable
   environment near the international border, does not generate too
   much stabilizing outflow, it is possible that this could become
   supportive of a swath of strong to severe wind gusts, aided by
   evaporative cooling in a deeply mixed sub-cloud layer with 40-50 F
   temperature/dew point spreads.

   ...Northern Mid Atlantic through western New England...
   Along and ahead of a weak cold front, trailing a deepening surface
   low migrating northeastward toward the lower St. Lawrence Valley,
   boundary-layer destabilization will probably be slowed, if not
   inhibited, by weak mid-level lapse rates and early day cloud
   cover/precipitation,  However, strengthening deep-layer wind fields
   and shear will potentially become more supportive of organized
   thunderstorm development with a risk for damaging wind gusts and
   perhaps a tornado or two, if pre-frontal breaks in cloud cover can
   allow for sufficient destabilization with daytime heating.
