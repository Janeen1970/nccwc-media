   ...THERE IS AN ENHANCED RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PORTIONS
   OF THE UPPER MIDWEST AND GREAT LAKES...

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS NORTH
   CAROLINA AND FAR SOUTHERN VIRGINIA...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Severe thunderstorms are expected across portions of the Upper
   Midwest and Great Lakes vicinity, especially late this afternoon and
   tonight.  Swaths of damaging winds, large hail, and a few tornadoes
   will all be possible. Isolated damaging wind gusts may also occur
   over parts of the Carolinas and southern Virginia.

   ...Upper Midwest/Great Lakes...
   Radar/IR satellite composite this morning shows a decaying MCS over
   far northern IA into west-central WI with warm-advection-related
   storms moving east-southeast atop the MCS cold pool.  This
   convective activity is occurring downstream of a seasonably potent 
   mid-level shortwave trough and associated jet streak.  

   Surface analysis this morning places a front over the mid MS Valley
   extending north-northwest and intersecting an outflow boundary over
   southwest MN.  A surface low over western ON is forecast to develop
   east across north-central ON as an attendant cold front pushes
   southeast across the Upper Midwest.  The front over the mid- to
   upper MS Valley will advance northeast as a warm front and the warm
   sector will become increasingly narrow with northward extent in the
   northern WI vicinity.  

   A warm and seasonably moist airmass will advect northeast into WI in
   wake of the morning showers/thunderstorms, while a plume of elevated
   mixed-layer air overspreads the region from the west.  Models
   generally show upper 60s to lower/middle 70s F surface dewpoints
   contributing to a very unstable boundary layer by mid-late
   afternoon.  However, considerable uncertainty remains regarding
   details with this forecast update due in part to the morning
   convection and downstream destabilization/hazard specifics for later
   this afternoon/evening. 

   It is plausible the initial storms that develop later this afternoon
   will quickly organize into supercells given strong deep-layer shear
   and ample buoyancy ahead of the cold front.  In agreement with prior
   forecast thinking, a corridor of large hail and tornado risk will
   extend from Iowa across Wisconsin into Upper MI.  Forecast soundings
   show elongated hodographs with 35-50 kt 700 mb flow, which will act
   to enlarge hodographs and perhaps support a couple of strong
   tornadoes.  However, will defer any potential severe changes to
   later outlook updates if greater confidence and morning convective
   details are resolved.  Eventually, a few clusters will probably
   evolve this evening, with a continued damaging wind and some tornado
   risk persisting southeastward before eventually waning overnight.  

   ...Carolinas and southern Virginia...
   Strong heating to the south of a west-east oriented stalled front
   will contribute to moderate buoyancy by early to mid afternoon. 
   Rich low-level moisture (surface dewpoints in the 70s F) and moist
   tropospheric profiles will yield high PW and the potential for
   water-loaded downdrafts with the more intense thunderstorm cores. 
   Scattered thunderstorms with an isolated to scattered wind-damage
   threat will probably focus during the 18-00 UTC period.  

   ...Florida...
   Tropical Depression Two is forecast to slowly intensify across the
   northeast Gulf of America today and tonight (refer to NHC's latest
   forecast details).  Model guidance shows some gradual strengthening
   of 2-3km AGL flow, especially across the western Florida Peninsula. 
   Yet, forecast soundings imply the possibility for transient
   supercell structures will likely be limited/low due to substantially
   weaker flow in the midlevels---precluding a low-severe highlight at
   this time.
