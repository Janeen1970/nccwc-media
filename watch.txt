   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1234 AM CDT Wed Aug 26 2026

   Valid 261200Z - 271200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS FOR PARTS OF THE
   CENTRAL HIGH PLAINS...OZARKS/MID-SOUTH...AND MIDWEST...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated to widely scattered severe storms are expected across parts
   of the central and southern High Plains, Great Lakes into the Ohio
   Valley, and parts of the Ozarks to Lower Mississippi Valley.
   Damaging/severe gusts are the main threat across these regions,
   though large hail is also possible, particularly across the High
   Plains.

   ...Lower MI into parts of northwest OH...IN/IL...Eastern MO...
   A mid/upper trough will move from the Upper Great Lakes/Midwest
   toward the Lower Great Lakes/Ohio Valley through the period. A
   surface low will move across Ontario, as a trailing cold front moves
   across parts of the Great Lakes and Ohio/mid MS Valleys. Moderate to
   locally strong destabilization is generally expected along/ahead of
   the front from parts of MO/IL/IN into lower MI, though early-day
   convection and cloudiness could limit heating/destabilization with
   northward extent. Scattered storm development is expected along the
   front during the afternoon, which will spread east-southeastward
   into the early evening. 

   Effective shear of generally 30-40 kt will support some storm
   organization, with a few supercells and/or organized line segments
   possible. Damaging winds and large hail could accompany the
   strongest storms. If any supercells can be sustained, then some
   tornado potential cannot be ruled out, especially from northern IN
   into parts of lower MI, where some enhancement to low-level
   shear/SRH is currently forecast.

   ...Parts of the Ozarks into the Lower MS Valley...
   Guidance varies regarding potential for morning convection across
   southeast MO. If substantial early-day convection does develop, then
   a loosely organized cluster could evolve and move southeastward into
   parts of western TN and northern MS/AL, with wind-damage potential.
   Otherwise, scattered storm development is expected from southern MO
   into AR and eastern OK by late afternoon, potentially aided by an
   MCV that may emerge from parts of the southern Plains. Deep-layer
   shear will be marginally supportive of storm organization, and
   relatively large PW and buoyancy will support potential for
   downbursts and loosely organized clusters with a wind-damage threat.
   Isolated hail also cannot be ruled out with the strongest updrafts.
   Some guidance depicts maintenance of a southward-moving storm
   cluster into late tonight, resulting in some wind-damage potential
   spreading into parts of southern LA/MS before the end of the period.

   ...Central/southern High Plains...
   Across the central High Plains, relatively moist low-level easterly
   flow and diurnal heating will result in moderate destabilization
   this afternoon. Scattered storm development is expected by late
   afternoon, with sufficient effective shear within the northwesterly
   flow regime for organized multicells and possibly a few supercells.
   Large hail (potentially approaching 2 inches in diameter) and
   localized severe gusts will be the primary threats. Some
   clustering/upscale growth is possible during the evening, which
   could result in maintenance of a severe-wind threat into parts of
   western KS. 

   For the southern High Plains, deep-layer shear will generally be
   weaker compared to areas farther north, with relatively disorganized
   convection expected. However, storm development within a very warm
   and well-mixed environment will support potential for at least
   localized severe gusts as convection spreads from eastern NM into
   the TX/OK Panhandles. Depending on trends regarding storm coverage
   and clustering within this regime, greater wind probabilities may
   eventually be needed.
