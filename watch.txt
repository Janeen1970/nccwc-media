  Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1152 AM CDT Mon Aug 17 2026

   Valid 171630Z - 181200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS THE
   NORTH-CENTRAL PLAINS/UPPER MIDWEST AND SOUTHERN APPALACHIANS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms with severe wind gusts and large hail are expected
   this afternoon and evening across parts of the central and northern
   Plains. Damaging gusts will also be possible in parts of the
   southern Appalachians to the North Carolina Piedmont, and possibly
   across portions of the Mid-South and southern Plains.

   ...Northern/Central Plains...
   Weak height falls are expected regionally with a seasonally
   prominent strengthening of northwesterly flow aloft. A surface low
   will shift eastward and an attendant cold front will push
   southward/southeastward across eastern Montana, North Dakota and
   toward west/northwest Minnesota. The airmass preceding this front
   will be moist and strongly unstable, with dewpoints in the upper 60s
   and MLCAPE over 2500 J/kg. Multiple areas of initial development are
   expected, including along the front from eastern Dakotas into
   western Minnesota, and in the vicinity of the pre-frontal trough
   and/or front over the NE Panhandle vicinity, southern South Dakota,
   and southeast Montana/far eastern Wyoming.

   Mid/Late-afternoon storm development in each of these areas will
   progress into zones of greater buoyancy by evening. Some initial
   supercells capable of large hail are expected, especially given the
   strength of the deep-layer wind profiles, while steep low-level
   lapse rates and high cloud bases will support strong wind gust
   potential, especially with the possibility that storms may
   cluster/organize this evening across Nebraska/southern South Dakota,
   and possibly across Siouxland and western Minnesota. 

   ...Central/Southern Appalachians into the Piedmont...
   Within a very moist airmass, renewed thunderstorm development is
   expected along the southern flank of a remnant thunderstorm cluster
   and in vicinity of the higher terrain/mountains this afternoon.
   Vertical shear will not be overly strong, but moderate/strong
   buoyancy and steep low-level lapse rates will still support the
   potential for damaging wind gusts. These strong outflows could
   promote the development of several
   forward-propagating/southeast-moving line segments with a persistent
   potential for wind damage, at least through early evening.

   ...Oklahoma/Arkansas to Mid-South...
   Aided by an MCV, a small cluster of storms with a morning history of
   occasional severe-caliber winds continues at midday across northwest
   Arkansas. For additional short-term details, see Mesoscale
   Discussion 2010. While not certain or perhaps likely, a general
   eastward-persistence of this cluster could occur. Additional
   isolated storm development may occur regionally later this afternoon
   along the remnant outflow and/or front, with steep low-level lapse
   rates and ample buoyancy supportive of damaging downburst potential
   through around sunset.
