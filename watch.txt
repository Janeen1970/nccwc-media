  Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1129 AM CDT Sat Sep 19 2026

   Valid 191630Z - 201200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PARTS OF
   THE CENTRAL PLAINS AND CENTRAL HIGH PLAINS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms with scattered severe wind gusts and large hail are
   possible this afternoon and evening across parts of the central
   Plains.

   ...Ohio Valley...
   Recent surface analysis places a weak low over northern IN, along
   the northern end of an outflow boundary that is pushing
   southward/south-southeastward across central IL and central IN.
   Airmass south of this boundary is very moist, with dewpoints
   generally in the upper 60s to low 70s. Filtered heating is
   anticipated across this region today, with the resulting warm and
   moist conditions supporting moderate buoyancy. The strongest
   buoyancy is expected from southern IN into southwest OH and adjacent
   far northern KY. The surging character of the outflow combined with
   limited deep-layer vertical shear results in uncertainty regarding
   how thunderstorms will evolve this afternoon, with at least some
   potential for a predominance of undercut, poorly organized updrafts.
   That being said, potential also exists for just enough
   buoyancy/shear balance for a few stronger, more persistent bowing
   clusters. Steep low-level lapse rates will promote strong to severe
   gusts with any more persistent line segments that develop. 

   ...Southern Appalachians/Southeast...
   Warm and moist conditions will support airmass destabilization and
   the potential of afternoon/evening thunderstorms across the region.
   The region is largely void of coherent surface features, so any
   convective initiation would likely be tied to mesoscale areas of
   low-level confluence. Widely scattered to scattered storms are
   forecast during the afternoon, and a few stronger water-loaded
   downdrafts capable of 50 to 60 mph gusts are possible.

   ...Central and Southern Plains/Lower Missouri Valley...
   Recent surface analysis places a low over southwest IA, with a cold
   front extending back southwestward through southwest KS and the
   central OK Panhandle into the northwest TX Panhandle. This front is
   expected to slowly push south while larger-scale mid-level troughing
   over the Great Basin and central Rockies gradually shifts eastward.
   A seasonably moist airmass is in place ahead of the cold front, and
   moderate buoyancy is expected as this airmass heats and destabilizes
   this afternoon. 

   Low-level convergence along the cold front, augmented by modest
   large-scale forcing for ascent, will support scattered storm
   development during the late afternoon and early evening as
   convective inhibition is locally eroded. Given adequate speed and
   directional shear, a few short-lived supercells are possible early
   in the convective cycle, particularly across KS. Large hail will be
   possible with the stronger updrafts. Thereafter, high-based storm
   character coupled with steep low-level lapse rates will support
   strong to severe gusts, including the potential for a few gusts of
   60 to 75 mph.

   ...Central High Plains...
   Low-level easterly flow across the central High Plains will help
   maintain at least upper 50s/low 60s dewpoints across the region this
   afternoon/evening. The low-amplitude shortwave trough currently
   moving through northern NV/UT is forecast to continue eastward,
   interacting with this moist and unstable airmass to support
   scattered thunderstorm development. With time, strengthening
   mid-level flow will help support upscale growth from a loosely
   organized band of a storms (including some supercells) to a more
   organized linear cluster. Some hail is possible early with the more
   cellular development, with wind gusts becoming more likely with
   time. There is some indication this activity may continue into
   western KS during the late evening and early overnight.
