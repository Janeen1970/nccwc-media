 Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1253 AM CDT Thu Sep 03 2026

   Valid 031200Z - 041200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS TODAY THROUGH
   TONIGHT ACROSS PARTS OF THE SOUTHERN GREAT LAKES INTO MID ATLANTIC
   AND PARTS OF THE NORTHERN GREAT PLAINS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Scattered strong thunderstorm development is likely today through
   tonight, perhaps including a few organizing clusters, posing a risk
   for severe wind and hail across parts of the southern Great Lakes
   into Mid Atlantic and portions of the northern Great Plains into
   Upper Midwest.

   ...Discussion...
   Large-scale mid-level troughing has overspread much of the Pacific
   coast through the Rockies.  However, models indicate that further
   eastward progress will slow, as at least one significant embedded
   short wave trough digs offshore and contributes to amplification of
   the larger-scale trough near and offshore of the Pacific coast.  As
   this occurs, downstream ridging is forecast to build across the
   northern Great Plains/middle Missouri Valley vicinity by late
   tonight, in the wake of a short wave trough now emerging from the
   Great Basin and forecast to progress north-northeast of the northern
   Rockies through the eastern Canadian Prairies and northwestern
   Ontario.  Farther east, it appears that mid-level troughing and an
   embedded remnant low will slowly dig southeast of western
   Quebec/southern Ontario through the northern Atlantic Seaboard.

   A surface cold front accompanying the eastern Canadian perturbation
   is preceded by considerable ongoing thunderstorm activity
   overspreading the northern Mid Atlantic through Lake Erie vicinity.
   It appears that an outflow boundary associated with this convection
   may become quasi-stationary across parts of southeastern
   Pennsylvania/New Jersey, before the initially stalled upstream
   front, across southern portions of the Great Lakes vicinity into
   Upper Midwest, advances southeast of the lower Great Lakes through
   much of the northern Mid Atlantic and upper Ohio Valley by late
   tonight.  

   ...Southern Great Lakes into northern Mid Atlantic...
   The initially stalled composite outflow boundary and surface front
   appear likely to provide the focus for renewed thunderstorm
   development later today through tonight.  Across the southeastern
   Wisconsin through southern Lower Michigan/northern Indiana and Ohio
   vicinity, it appears that the corridor of stronger destabilization
   along this boundary will remain in relatively close proximity to the
   southern fringe of the westerlies.  East-southeastward into the
   northern Mid Atlantic this remains more unclear.  

   Regardless, where the seasonably moist boundary layer becomes
   strongly heated, thermodynamic profiles a bit further aloft into
   mid-levels, aided by elevated mixed-layer air, will remain
   characterized by steep lapse rates of temperature and potential
   wet-bulb temperature.  This will promote the risk for strong
   downbursts and evolving cold pools capable of producing strong to
   severe wind gusts in developing thunderstorm activity.

   There appears potential for one or two organizing clusters across
   and east-southeast of the southern Great Lakes region this afternoon
   into tonight.  It is possible that severe wind probabilities could
   still be increased further, once forcing for convection and the
   potential negative impacts of early day convection become clearer.

   ...Northern Great Plains into Upper Midwest...
   Forcing for ascent, near and downstream of the short wave emerging
   from the Great Basin, appears likely to overspread northern/eastern
   North Dakota into northwestern Minnesota during peak boundary-layer
   instability this afternoon and evening.  Latest convection-allowing
   guidance suggests that this will provide support for intensifying
   thunderstorm development, which may include a few supercells in the
   leading warm advection regime, ahead of a possible upscale growing
   cluster near the crest of the building larger-scale mid-level
   ridging.  Aided by pronounced veering of lower through mid-level
   wind fields, deep-layer shear appears conducive to the risk for
   large hail, in the presence of thermodynamic profiles with steep
   mid-level lapse rates and large CAPE in mid/upper levels.  A few
   strong downbursts also appear possible.
