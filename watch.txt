
   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   0757 AM CDT Wed Sep 30 2026

   Valid 301300Z - 011200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PORTIONS OF
   THE SOUTHERN AND CENTRAL PLAINS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms capable of severe wind gusts/some hail and a brief
   tornado are expected across the southern Plains this afternoon into
   tonight including portions of west Texas into southwest Oklahoma.
   The potential for a few tornadoes and localized wind damage will
   also exist across Kansas and possibly far southeast Nebraska.

   ...Central/southern Plains...
   A mid/upper-level low will move from the southern Rockies toward the
   central High Plains through tonight. A surface low initially over
   the southern High Plains is forecast to move only slowly eastward
   through tonight. A warm front extending northeast of the surface low
   may move slowly northward through the day, though persistent clouds
   and precipitation may hinder its advance. 

   A rather strong low-level jet (50+ kt diurnally) is forecast to
   slowly shift northeastward across parts of central/eastern Kansas
   into western Missouri and southeast Nebraska/southwest Iowa by
   evening. This will help to maintain strong wind profiles in the
   vicinity of the central Plains effective warm front. Destabilization
   near the warm front may remain weak due to limited diurnal heating
   and weak mid-level lapse rates.

   While uncertainties remain regarding the disposition of persistent
   early day precipitation and cloud cover, will defer to the
   anticipated strength of 0-1km/0-3km AGL winds and related
   supercell-conducive profiles. Have upgraded portions of
   central/eastern Kansas into far southeast Nebraska with the notion
   that multiple tornadoes are a viable possibility, even if
   destabilization remains muted later today.

   Farther south, the coverage and persistence of early-day convection
   across the southern Plains remains somewhat uncertain, though there
   may be a tendency for convection to weaken and/or shift northward by
   early afternoon. The greatest confidence in substantial diurnal
   destabilization is from southwest Oklahoma into northwest Texas and
   the Edwards Plateau, where heating of a very moist airmass will
   result in MLCAPE increasing to near/above 1500 J/kg, despite poor
   mid-level lapse rates. Renewed storm development is expected from
   late afternoon into the evening, in advance of the approaching
   mid/upper-level low. Moderate mid-level flow will provide sufficient
   deep-layer shear for storm organization. 

   While supercells will be possible initially, generally
   front-parallel flow and increasing storm coverage will likely lead
   to more of a linear mode with time. Strong to severe gusts appear to
   be the most likely hazard, though isolated hail also appears
   possible across southwest Texas, where somewhat stronger
   destabilization may occur. Also, while low-level flow will be weaker
   compared to areas farther north, some increase in effective SRH is
   expected during the evening, which could support a localized tornado
   threat within the very moist environment. An isolated severe threat
   could persist into parts of central Texas/Oklahoma overnight, though
   diminishing buoyancy with time and eastward extent should result in
   an eventual weakening trend.

   Elsewhere, a few strong to locally severe lower-topped storms could
   occur across the south-central High Plains pending sufficient
   destabilization.
