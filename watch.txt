Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1227 AM CDT Fri Jul 31 2026

   Valid 311200Z - 011200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PARTS OF
   THE OZARKS AND MID MISSISSIPPI VALLEY...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms with severe wind gusts and hail will be possible this
   afternoon and evening across parts of the Ozarks and mid Mississippi
   Valley. Isolated severe gusts will also be possible in parts of the
   southern Plains and from east-central Colorado into northeastern New
   Mexico.

   ...Ozarks/Mid Mississippi Valley...
   At mid-levels, a positively tilted shortwave trough will move
   southeastward across the mid to upper Mississippi Valley. An
   associated cold front will advance southeastward into the western
   Ozarks. Ahead of the front, an axis of moisture will be in place
   with surface dewpoints in the 70s F. As surface temperatures warm,
   moderate instability will develop along and near the moist axis,
   with MLCAPE forecast to peak in the 2500 to 3500 J/kg range during
   the afternoon. In addition to the instability, mid-level flow will
   be westerly at 30 to 35 knots, which will contribute to moderate
   deep-layer shear over much of the moist sector. This will result a
   mixed mode with supercells and organized line segments both
   possible. Along the instability axis, 0-3 km lapse rates are
   forecast to peak near 7 C/km which will support a threat for severe
   wind gusts. The threat could persist into the early to mid evening
   as storms move southeastward across the mid Mississippi Valley.

   ...Southern Plains/East-central Colorado/Northeast New Mexico...
   Northwesterly mid-level flow will be in place across much of the
   central U.S. today. At the surface, a cold front will move southward
   into central Oklahoma and the Texas Panhandle by afternoon. An axis
   of moderate instability will develop ahead of the front as
   temperatures become hot in the mid to late afternoon. Increasing
   low-level convergence near the front will result in scattered
   thunderstorm development. These storms will move south-southeastward
   across the Red River Valley and southward across the Caprock of west
   Texas. Very steep low to mid-level lapse rates and large temperature
   dewpoint spreads exceeding 40 degrees F in some areas will result in
   a potential for isolated severe wind gusts.

   Further west into east-central Colorado and northeast New Mexico, an
   axis of instability will develop by afternoon in and near the
   foothills of the southern Rockies. Storms will form in the mid to
   late afternoon in the higher terrain and gradually move
   southeastward into the lower elevations. Steep low to mid-level
   lapse rates will support a potential for isolated severe gusts. Hail
   could also occur with the stronger updrafts.
