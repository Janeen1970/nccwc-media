
   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1237 AM CDT Tue Jul 28 2026

   Valid 281200Z - 291200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PARTS OF
   THE MID-ATLATIC AND CAROLINAS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms with potential for severe wind gusts and a tornado
   threat are expected today from parts of the Mid-Atlantic southward
   into the Carolinas. A few severe gusts and hail may also occur over
   parts of the central High Plains.

   ...Mid-Atlantic/Carolinas...
   An upper-level trough will dig south-southeastward into the eastern
   U.S. today, as an associated cold front moves into the central
   Appalachians. Ahead of the front, a moist and unstable airmass will
   be in place from the Mid-Atlantic southward into the Carolinas.
   Surface dewpoints in the lower to mid 70s F will contribute to
   moderate instability by afternoon over much of the region.
   Convection is expected to first develop around midday in the higher
   terrain of northern Virginia and Pennsylvania. These storms are
   forecast to expand in coverage, moving southward through the
   remainder of Virginia into North Carolina during the afternoon.

   The strongest instability is forecast from southeastern Virginia
   into central North Carolina, where MLCAPE could peak around 3000
   J/kg. RAP forecast soundings near the instability axis suggest that
   0-6 km shear will be in the 40 to 50 knot range, supportive of
   supercells. In addition, 0-3 km storm-relative helicity is forecast
   to reach near 225 m2/s2 by mid afternoon, with the low-level shear
   being amplified by the directional component. This should support an
   isolated tornado threat with supercells. Supercells and organized
   multicell line segments will also be capable of severe wind gusts.
   The potential for severe gusts could extend further west into the
   foothills of the Appalachians across west-central North Carolina and
   far northern South Carolina.

   ...Central High Plains...
   A mid-level ridge will be in place today across the High Plains, as
   a subtle shortwave trough moves eastward through the ridge. This
   feature along with topographic ascent will support scattered
   convective development during the afternoon. Thunderstorms will form
   in the higher terrain and move eastward into the lower elevations.
   Ahead of the storms, RAP forecast soundings at 21Z have MLCAPE in
   the 2000 to 2500 J/kg range with 0-6 km shear around 30 knots. This,
   combined with steep low-level lapse rates and surface-temperature
   dewpoint spreads near 30 degrees F should support a potential for
   isolated severe wind gusts. Hail will also be possible with the
   stronger cells.
