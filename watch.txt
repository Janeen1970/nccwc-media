Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   0738 AM CDT Thu Jul 23 2026

   Valid 231300Z - 241200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS WESTERN
   KANSAS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Widely scattered severe storms capable of severe wind gusts and
   large hail are possible over portions of the central and southern
   High Plains this afternoon and evening. More isolated occurrences of
   damaging wind are possible from the middle Texas coast into Deep
   South Texas, the eastern Carolinas, and parts of the Columbia Basin
   into the northern Intermountain region.

   ...Central/southern High Plains including western Kansas...
   Strong boundary-layer heating is forecast in the vicinity of a High
   Plains surface front and dryline this afternoon with temperatures
   reaching the mid 90s to around 105 F. The hot temperatures will
   combine with dewpoints in the mid 50s to mid 60s and modestly steep
   mid-level lapse rates to yield a narrow axis of moderate instability
   with MLCAPE of 1500-2500 J/kg across western Kansas southward into
   western Oklahoma and the eastern Texas Panhandle. Forcing for ascent
   preceding a shortwave trough is expected to contribute to isolated
   to widely scattered thunderstorms by mid-afternoon from the vicinity
   of the thermal low in southeast Colorado to along the front and
   dryline from northeast Colorado into the Oklahoma/Texas Panhandles.

   Forecast soundings along the front feature a vertically veering wind
   profile with 30-35 kt of deep-layer shear, which will be sufficient
   for at least episodic supercell structures with a risk
   for large hail. In contrast, the storms forming in the hot, deeply
   mixed air mass west of the boundary are more likely to produce
   sporadic severe wind gusts. A corridor of more concentrated severe
   wind gusts may materialize across portions of southwest Kansas by
   evening, particularly if organized cold pool development occurs with
   an evening time low-level jet increase.

   ...Middle Texas Coast to Deep South Texas...
   As Bertha continues westward over the northwest Gulf, a belt of
   enhanced mid-level flow will precede Bertha and spread atop a hot
   and moist boundary layer inland, with moderate instability
   developing by afternoon along the middle Texas coast. Similar to
   Wednesday, widely scattered thunderstorms are expected during the
   afternoon within an environment characterized by 30-40 kt of
   deep-layer shear, steep low-level lapse rates, and high PW content.
   As such, the potential will exist for locally damaging downburst
   winds as storms spread west-southwestward along the coast into Deep
   South Texas during the afternoon and evening.

   ...Columbia Basin/northern Intermountain West...
   Weak height falls are expected while mid/upper-level winds
   strengthen regionally ahead of an upper low off the coast of the
   Pacific Northwest. Daytime heating along favored terrain, in
   conjunction with the glancing influence of the passing disturbance,
   will aid in isolated to widely scattered thunderstorms by
   mid-afternoon within a moderately unstable and steep lapse rate
   environment. The strengthening upper-level winds will enhance
   vertical shear, supporting the potential for some storm organization
   with an associated risk for locally damaging wind gusts and
   marginally severe hail.

   ...Eastern Carolinas...
   A very moist air mass and cloud breaks to the south of
   front-associated convection early today across North Carolina should
   allow for at least moderate destabilization across parts of the
   coastal plain. This may result in a corridor of more concentrated
   thunderstorm development, near the intersection of the outflow from
   the morning storms with the sea-breeze boundary. Modest deep-layer
   shear will coincide with steepening low-level lapse rates and high
   PW content to yield locally intense wet microbursts capable of
   damaging winds this afternoon until around sunset.
