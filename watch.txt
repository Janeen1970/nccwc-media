   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1242 AM CDT Mon Sep 14 2026

   Valid 141200Z - 151200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS EASTERN NE
   AND WESTERN IA...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Scattered strong to severe thunderstorms are possible across parts
   of eastern Nebraska into western Iowa late this afternoon and
   evening.

   ...Eastern NE/western IA...
   A strong mid/upper-level trough will move eastward from the northern
   Plains into the Upper Great Lakes through tonight. A deepening
   surface low will move across the Canadian Prairies, while a
   secondary low may develop along a trailing cold front/surface trough
   across the central High Plains. This system will be accompanied by
   strengthening deep-layer flow from the central Plains into the Upper
   Midwest. 

   Extensive elevated convection will likely be ongoing at the start of
   the period from the mid Missouri Valley northward into the Dakotas
   and western MN. In the wake of this early-day convection, rich
   low-level moisture (with dewpoints in the 70s F) is forecast to
   stream northward into eastern NE and western IA. This moistening
   combined with relatively steep midlevel lapse rates will support
   moderate to strong destabilization by late afternoon, while
   low-level and deep-layer shear will become increasingly favorable
   with time. The resulting environment will become conditionally
   favorable for supercells capable of all severe hazards. 

   The strongest large-scale ascent will become focused north of the
   region by afternoon, and it remains uncertain if heating in the wake
   of morning convection will be sufficient to remove CINH within the
   most favorable environment. If a supercell can become established
   and move through this environment during the late afternoon/early
   evening, then large to very large hail, localized damaging gusts,
   and tornadoes will all be possible. While most guidance depicts
   storm development by early evening, 00Z CAM guidance continues to
   vary regarding the potential for sustained supercells, with
   relatively quick clustering possible. Even in the absence of
   sustained supercell development, the environment will be favorable
   for severe hail and damaging wind with any persistent clusters into
   the evening. 

   ...Western/north-central KS...
   Strong heating is expected near/south of a surface trough across
   parts of western/central KS this afternoon. Scattered storm
   development is expected by late afternoon from western into
   north-central KS. Deep-layer shear will be weaker with southwest
   extent, but steep low-level lapse rates and 25-35 kt of flow in the
   850-500 mb layer will support potential for one or more loosely
   organized clusters with at least localized severe-wind potential. 

   ...Four Corners region into AZ...
   Scattered storm development is expected later today from western CO
   and southeast UT into parts of AZ/NM, within an anomalously moist
   environment. The strongest mid/upper-level flow and deep-layer shear
   within this region is expected over parts of UT/CO, though it is
   uncertain if diurnal heating/destabilization will be sufficient for
   an organized severe threat in this area. Some threat for strong
   gusts and small to near-severe hail could evolve with the strongest
   storms. 

   Farther south, flow will be weaker across AZ, resulting in generally
   disorganized storms. Localized strong gusts will be possible,
   especially in areas where stronger pre-storm heating occurs during
   the afternoon.  

   ...Coastal NC...
   A cold front will move southward across NC through the day today.
   Large-scale ascent will generally be weak, though isolated storm
   development will be possible as the front encounters richer moisture
   and any sea breeze boundaries near the coast. Deep-layer shear
   within a northwesterly flow regime will be sufficient for organized
   convection, but poor midlevel lapse rates may limit updraft
   intensity and potential for an organized severe threat. Any
   sustained storms within this regime could produce locally gusty
   winds and small hail.
