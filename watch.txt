   .PREV DISCUSSION... /ISSUED 1130 AM CDT Fri Aug 28 2026/

   ...Maine...
   Morning surface analysis places a low over eastern Quebec, with a
   cold front extending southward from this low through northern NY
   before arcing more southwestward and becoming increasingly diffuse
   across the Upper OH Valley. A shortwave trough is forecast to
   progress through southern Quebec towards the St. Lawrence Valley
   today. The surface low is forecast to eject northeastward in
   response, with its attendant cold front sweeping eastward across ME.
   A moist airmass precedes this front, with current observations
   sampling mid to upper 60s dewpoints. This airmass should destabilize
   amid filtered daytime heating ahead of the front, although poor
   mid-level lapse will limit the overall buoyancy. Scattered
   thunderstorm development is anticipated as the cold front interacts
   with this modestly buoyant airmass. A few strong to severe storms
   are possible, but deep-layer shear largely parallel to the frontal
   boundary will contribute to a predominantly linear mode and a likely
   trend toward undercutting of new updrafts. Even so, any updrafts
   that can mature could organize, potentially becoming supercell
   storms capable of locally wind gusts and hail. 

   ...Mid-Atlantic into Georgia...
   Morning surface analysis places a low off the southern New England
   coast and another over southern NJ (near the ongoing thunderstorms).
   A weak front extends through these two lows, continuing
   southwestward across the Carolina Piedmont into northern GA. Widely
   scattered thunderstorms are expected to form along
   the front this afternoon, with slow-moving but strong storms
   possible. The stronger (but still modest) deep-layer shear will
   occur across VA and NC, with the stronger buoyancy displaced farther
   south across SC and GA. Even so, the expectation is that a
   combination of moderate CAPE, high PWAT values, and limited
   deep-layer shear will promote isolated strong storms capable of
   water-loaded downbursts.

   ...Great Basin through Wyoming..
   A belt of stronger mid-level flow currently extends from central CA
   through the northern Rockies between the upper troughing over
   western Canada and the upper ridging centered over the Four Corners.
   Low-amplitude shortwave troughs embedded within this enhanced flow,
   and associated ascent and mid-level moisture, will contribute to
   thunderstorm development this afternoon. A deeply mixed boundary
   layer is anticipated throughout much of this region, suggesting any
   of the stronger, more persistent storms could produce damaging
   gusts. Highest storm coverage is expected from eastern NV into
   central/northern UT, resulting in a relatively greater threat for
   damaging gusts within this corridor.  

   ...Dakotas into western Minnesota and northern Iowa...
   There could potentially be two rounds of convection across the
   northern Plains today. The first will be with any of the high-based
   convection from WY as it spreads eastward into western
   Dakotas, where greater low-level moisture and buoyancy will be in
   place. The greater buoyancy could result in reintensification and a
   few bowing structures capable of producing locally damaging wind
   gusts and hail.  

   Additional warm-air advection associated development is then
   possible later this evening as the low-level jet strengthens over
   the Plains. These elevated storms should develop within an
   environment characterized by moderate buoyancy and deep-layer shear,
   suggesting a few supercells capable of large hail are possible
   initially. Over time, some upscale growth is possible, which could
   result in a low-probability risk for damaging gusts over southwest
   MN and adjacent northwest IA early tomorrow morning.
