   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS FROM CENTRAL OHIO
   FROM WESTERN NEW YORK...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated strong to severe thunderstorms are possible across portions
   of the central Plains to the Ohio Valley today. A more organized
   severe weather threat is expected from eastern Ohio to western New
   York.

   ...Synopsis...
   Early-morning satellite imagery show broad upper troughing from the
   Pacific Northwest/British Columbia through the Upper Great Lakes and
   Ontario. Several shortwave troughs are embedded within this larger
   upper troughing, including one moving into northern MN and another
   farther north across far northwestern Ontario. A belt of moderate to
   strong mid-level westerly/southwesterly flow extends between the
   base of these two waves. Expectation is the southern wave to move
   quickly eastward through the Upper Great Lakes and Ontario,
   accompanied by mid-level jet max. 

   Surface analysis places a occluded low over far northwestern
   Ontario, with a cold front extending southward into western Upper MI
   and then back southwestward through central NE. An outflow-augmented
   pre-frontal trough precedes this front, extending from a low in
   southwest Lower MI to another low over northeast KS to another over
   far southwest KS/far southeast CO. Both the cold front and
   pre-frontal trough are expected to progress modestly
   southward/southeastward today. Two corridors of relatively higher
   thunderstorm coverage are anticipated this afternoon/evening, one
   from eastern OH into western PA/western NY and the other farther
   southwest from the Lower MO Valley into the TX/OK Panhandles.

   ...Central Ohio into western New York... 
   Moist low-levels are already in place from the OH Valley into
   western PA and NY, with recent surface analysis sampling upper 60s
   dewpoints in central OH decreasing to the low/mid 60s across western
   PA and western NY. This moist airmass is expected to destabilize
   amid modest low-level moisture advection and filtered insolation,
   ahead of a convectively augmented vorticity maximum. With the cold
   front still well to the northwest, this vorticity maximum will be
   the primary impetus for thunderstorm development, augmenting the
   weak low-level confluence along the weak pre-frontal trough. A
   predominately linear/clustered storm mode is anticipated, but
   strengthening mid-level flow will support effective bulk shear from
   30 to 40 kt, which could support some more persist/organized line
   segments. Damaging gusts are the primary risk with any of these more
   organized segments. Low-level hodographs do show some notable
   curative, particularly from western PA into western NY after 00Z,
   amid a strengthening low-level jet. As such, there is a
   low-probability tornado risk is these areas.

   ...Lower OH and MO Valleys into the TX/OK Panhandles...
   Thunderstorm coverage is gradually expected to increase across the
   region this afternoon and evening, beginning near a surface low move
   across the Lower MO Valley before expanding back southwestward along
   the southward-progressing front. The stronger vertical shear will be
   displace north of the front, and a largely disorganized,
   outflow-dominant storm mode is anticipated. Slightly better flow
   aloft may favor a more focused cluster across the Mid-Mississippi
   Valley into western Kentucky/Tennessee. Steep low-level lapse rates
   and high cloud bases could support strong gusts from the Lower MO
   Valley into the TX/OK Panhandles.

   ...Southern AZ...
   Afternoon thunderstorms are expected over the higher terrain near
   the southern AZ/NM border. There is some potential this activity
   progresses westward into the lower elevations of southern AZ.
   However, upper ridging will be centered over this region and
   vertical shear will be weak, resulting in slow storm motion and
   disorganized, outflow-dominant structures. A few strong gusts could
   result from any more persistent updrafts.
