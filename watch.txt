  Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1257 AM CDT Wed Sep 02 2026

   Valid 021200Z - 031200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS NORTHERN
   AND CENTRAL MT AND FROM THE LOWER GREAT LAKES TO THE MID ATLANTIC...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Severe thunderstorms are possible across portions of the eastern
   Great Lakes to the Mid-Atlantic today and tonight. Damaging wind
   gusts are the main threat, though some large hail and a few
   tornadoes may also occur. Scattered severe gusts and more sporadic
   occurrences of large hail appear possible from the northern Rockies
   into northern High Plains Wednesday afternoon into night.

   ...Eastern Great Lakes, Mid Atlantic...
   Several low-amplitude impulses embedded within a belt of moderate
   mid-level northwest flow of 40-60 kt will move from the lower Great
   Lakes into the northeast and Mid Atlantic today and tonight. A
   surface low over James bay will trail a slow moving cold front to
   the southwest across lower ON, the lakes and into MI/OH with a
   second backdoor front stalled across the eastern Hudson Valley and
   southern new England. Within the warm sector, a very moist air mass
   will be in place over much of the Northeast contributing to large
   buoyancy and broad, but uncertain potential for severe thunderstorms
   much of the day.

   A complex convective scenario is expected across the region
   modulated by several rounds of thunderstorms beginning early this
   morning. Convection associated with a lead shortwave trough exiting
   southern ON will likely support scattered storms along the front
   across the lower Great Lakes and NY this morning. Isolated large
   hail and damaging winds are possible with these storms as they move
   southeastward and encounter a gradually destabilizing air mass
   across southern PA and possibly into VA/MD.

   Air mass recovery is expected behind this initial condition as the
   primary upper trough moves from WI and lower MI into western NY/PA
   later in the afternoon. Steep mid-level lapse rates of 7-7.5 C/km
   atop boundary layer dewpoints in the upper 60s to low 70s will aide
   in destabilization with upwards of 2000-3000+ J/kg of MLCAPE by
   afternoon from lower MI into western parts of NY and PA. Storm
   development is expected ahead of the approaching trough and cold
   front as well as on any residual outflow from the morning storms. A
   mix of supercells and line segments is expected with all hazards
   possible.

   A greater tornado risk may evolve in the evening across southern NY
   and central PA, contingent upon air mass recovery in the wake of the
   prior storms and presence of a more supercellular mode. NAM/RAP
   forecast soundings do show some elongation of low-level hodographs,
   and any modifying old pool or outflow boundary could further enhance
   low-level shear profiles favorable for tornadoes. Higher tornado
   probabilities were considered, but uncertainty on the aforementioned
   factors remains large.

   ...MT/ND...
   A compact but strong shortwave trough is forecast to move rapidly
   northeastward from the Great Basin to the northern Rockies today and
   tonight. 35-50 kt mid-level flow will accompany this feature and
   overspread a modestly moist, but warm and unstable air mass across
   much of central and eastern MT. Ascent from the approaching
   shortwave trough will aid in the development of thunderstorms across
   the higher terrain of western MT through the afternoon. Despite
   modest buoyancy, steep lapse rate profiles and a deep, well-mixed
   boundary layer, will enhance severe-wind-gust potential with these
   storms. 30-40 kt of deep-layer shear will promote organization and
   clustering with some guidance showing a fast-moving line developing
   and moving northeastward through the later afternoon/early evening.
   Scattered severe gusts are likely, with peak intensity of 65-80 mph
   possible.

   Additional storms are possible across portions of eastern MT into
   the evening within a zone of warm advection along a nocturnally
   strengthening low-level jet. Isolated large hail appears to be the
   primary hazard with those storms.

   ...WY...
   Some model guidance shows high-based showers and thunderstorms
   developing near the southern fringes of the northern Rockies trough
   across WY this afternoon. Modest low-level moisture/buoyancy may
   limit convective intensity, but sporadic strong outflow gusts are
   possible given a very deeply mixed boundary layer.

   ...Mid MO Valley into IA and southern MN...
   An intensifying low-level jet will likely support an increase in
   thunderstorms late this evening/overnight across the MO valley and
   parts of the Midwest. Moderate to strong elevated instability and
   sufficient vertical shear may exist for a few stronger storms
   capable of marginal severe hail. However, overall forcing for ascent
   is modest and with building mid-level heights, confidence in a
   broader organized severe threat is low.
