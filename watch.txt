   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   0750 AM CDT Sun Aug 02 2026

   Valid 021300Z - 031200Z

   ...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS THE EAST
   AS WELL AS PARTS OF THE SOUTHWEST AND NORTHERN PLAINS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Scattered strong to locally severe thunderstorm development is
   expected this afternoon and evening across parts of the lower Great
   Lakes and upper Ohio Valley into the middle and southern Atlantic
   Piedmont. Damaging winds are the primary hazard, although a tornado
   could occur.

   ...Eastern States...
   The large-scale positive-tilt upper trough will continue to drift
   eastward regionally, with the leading edge of height falls generally
   parallel to the Appalachians. Ahead of a cold front, early day cloud
   cover, precipitation, and otherwise generally weak
   lower/mid-tropospheric lapse rates will be factors that tend to
   limit overall boundary-layer destabilization.

   Breaks in cloud cover into this afternoon will eventually allow for
   widely scattered to scattered intensifying thunderstorm development
   into mid/late afternoon. This may focus within a pre-cold frontal
   corridor across the Allegheny Plateau/upper Ohio Valley into lower
   Great Lakes vicinity, where it could be enhanced by 20-40 kt
   south-southwesterly flow in the 850-500 mb layer, and within
   somewhat more modest flow off the higher terrain in vicinity of the
   lee troughing across the Piedmont.

   With at least modest CAPE in excess of 1000 J/kg, and potential for
   heavy precipitation loading in the presence of seasonably high
   moisture content, localized strong downbursts may generate a few
   damaging surface gusts. A brief tornado threat may also exist across
   parts of upper Ohio Valley toward the Mid-Atlantic given sufficient
   low-level shear/SRH in the presence of a moist boundary layer.

   ...North Dakota/northwest Minnesota...
   The extent and likelihood of sustained deep convective development
   remain uncertain. Some guidance has suggested that there may be at
   least attempts at deep convective development late this afternoon
   near the weak pre-cold frontal surface troughing. However, the
   potential for sustained thunderstorm activity remains unclear due to
   limited low-level moisture beneath very warm and dry mid-levels,
   associated with an elevated mixed-layer overspreading the region
   today. A somewhat better potential for thunderstorm development may
   await increasing forcing for ascent with the approach of the
   upstream shortwave trough later tonight. In either case, some risk
   for hail and severe winds will exist pending development across the
   region.

   ...Southeast Arizona/far southwest New Mexico...
   Residual cloud cover and widely scattered convection should clear
   relatively early today, with renewed diurnal destabilization across
   southeast Arizona and nearby southwest New Mexico. At least widely
   scattered thunderstorms should develop over the mountains/higher
   terrain of the Arizona/New Mexico border vicinity, with
   southwestward storm persistence across southeast Arizona aided by
   moderately strong northeasterly winds. A few instances of
   severe-caliber wind gusts/blowing dust are possible this afternoon
   through early evening.
