Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1229 AM CDT Fri Jul 24 2026

   Valid 241200Z - 251200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS FOR
   CENTRAL/EASTERN ND INTO PARTS OF NORTHERN MN...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Severe thunderstorms capable of large to very large hail, damaging
   wind, and possibly a tornado are forecast during the afternoon and
   evening across portions of North Dakota into northern Minnesota.

   ...Dakotas into northern MN...
   Within an amplifying upper-level pattern, multiple low-amplitude
   midlevel shortwave troughs are forecast to move east-southeastward
   from the northern Rockies toward ND and northern MN through the
   period. A surface trough will trail southwestward from a deep
   surface cyclone that will move from northern MB/ON towards Hudson
   Bay. A plume of relatively steep midlevel lapse rates will
   overspread a warm and moist boundary layer along/east of the surface
   trough, with MLCAPE increasing above 2000 J/kg across parts of ND
   into northwest MN by late afternoon. Strengthening mid/upper-level
   west-northwesterly flow will result in favorable deep-layer shear,
   resulting in a conditionally favorable environment for severe
   storms.

   Guidance generally suggests that widely scattered storms will
   develop along the surface trough by late afternoon across eastern
   ND. Elongated hodographs and favorable instability will initially
   favor supercell development, though some clustering will be possible
   with time into the evening. Large to very large hail will be the
   primary initial hazard. Some tornado potential could evolve with
   persistent supercells, as low-level shear gradually increases from
   late afternoon into the evening. Any organized clustering could
   result in localized corridors of greater severe-wind potential. Some
   severe threat could persist as storms move into a larger portion of
   northern/central MN through late evening, with a general weakening
   trend expected late in the period due to increasing CINH and
   weakening buoyancy. 

   Farther northwest, some guidance depicts isolated supercell
   development west of the surface trough across the Canadian Prairies
   during the late afternoon, which could then move into parts of
   northern/central ND within a northwesterly flow regime. This
   scenario is more uncertain, but 5% wind/hail probabilities have been
   expanded westward in response to this potential. 

   ...Coastal Carolinas vicinity...
   Relatively strong heating of a richly moist airmass (PW above 2
   inches) will result in the development of moderate buoyancy across
   parts of the Coastal Carolinas and southeast GA, in the vicinity of
   a surface front. Scattered storm development is expected by
   early/mid afternoon. Deep-layer flow will generally be weak, but
   locally damaging downbursts and outflow winds will be possible
   within the warm and very moist environment.

   ...Southern High Plains vicinity...
   A nearly stationary front draped from eastern NM into the TX/OK
   Panhandles is expected to become a focus for thunderstorm
   development by late afternoon or early evening. Buoyancy is forecast
   to remain modest, and deep-layer flow will be weak, so organized
   storm potential currently appears low. However, strong
   boundary-layer heating/mixing will support potential for localized
   downbursts. While magnitude and coverage of the threat currently
   appear rather limited, wind probabilities may eventually be needed,
   if confidence increases in more than very localized severe-wind
   potential. 

   ...Parts of the Great Basin into the Northern Rockies...
   Widely scattered high-based storm development is expected this
   afternoon from parts of the Great Basin into the northern Rockies,
   along the periphery of an amplifying upper ridge over the Four
   Corners region. Buoyancy and deep-layer shear are both expected to
   remain weak, but strong to locally severe gusts will be possible
   within the hot and well-mixed regime. Wind probabilities may
   eventually be needed, if confidence increases in more than a very
   localized/transient severe-gust threat.
