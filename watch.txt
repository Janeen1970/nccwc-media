   
   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1135 AM CDT Sat Sep 12 2026

   Valid 121630Z - 131200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS THE
   SOUTH-CENTRAL PLAINS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated to scattered severe thunderstorms capable of producing
   large to very large hail and severe/damaging winds may occur across
   parts of the south-central Plains into the Ozarks this afternoon and
   evening.

   ...Central/Southern Plains to the Ozarks...
   Scattered mainly elevated thunderstorms continue at midday across
   the region, including the strongest/occasionally-severe storms
   across southeast Nebraska into northeast Kansas that are occurring
   near/just behind a southeastward-moving cold front. Some severe hail
   will remain possible as these storms continue southeastward on the
   edge of the primary instability reservoir. 

   This front, augmented by early day outflows, will otherwise serve as
   a focus for more robust thunderstorm development this
   afternoon/evening as well. With a rather moist low-level airmass
   forecast to be present along/south of the front, current
   expectations are for a narrow zone of moderate to strong instability
   (MLCAPE 2000-3500 J/kg) to develop this afternoon from the eastern
   Oklahoma/Texas Panhandles across southern Kansas and northern
   Oklahoma. Weak low-level winds will veer to west-northwesterly and
   strengthen some with height through mid/upper levels, supporting
   around 30-40 kt of deep-layer shear and updraft organization. While
   large-scale ascent will remain fairly weak/nebulous across the
   southern/central Plains, most guidance shows at least isolated
   robust thunderstorm development along the front by 21-23Z as
   convective temperatures are reached.

   Any sustained supercells would pose a threat for large to very large
   hail, with steep mid-level lapse rates and ample instability in the
   hail-growth zone supporting potential for 2+ inch
   diameter hail on an isolated basis. Somewhat higher-based convection
   is expected from the eastern Oklahoma/Texas Panhandles into
   southwest/south-central Kansas and northern Oklahoma, where a very
   well-mixed boundary layer with steep low/mid-level lapse rates
   will promote an environment that supports severe-caliber downdraft
   winds. Some gusts could exceed 75 mph. A degree of storm clustering
   could occur into this evening.

   ...Upper Ohio Valley...
   A regional pocket of relatively cloud-free skies at midday will
   contribute to filtered diurnal heating and moderate instability
   across the region. Thunderstorms will further increase in coverage
   and intensity through the afternoon, aided by forcing for ascent
   associated with an approaching weak mid-level shortwave trough.
   While mid-level lapse rates will remain poor, sufficient deep-layer
   shear coupled with modest steepening of low-level lapse rates
   suggest some risk for occasional damaging winds with any loosely
   organized clusters/line segments that develop.
