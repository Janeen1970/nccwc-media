 Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   0748 AM CDT Sat Jul 25 2026

   Valid 251300Z - 261200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS MONTANA AND
   SOUTH DAKOTA...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Storms with scattered severe wind gusts are possible across parts of
   Montana and South Dakota this afternoon and evening.

   ...Montana to Dakotas...
   A prominent upper ridge will persist over much of the
   Southwest/central CONUS through tonight, with very hot temperatures
   expected this afternoon from Montana into the Dakotas. Isolated to
   scattered storms within the hot and well-mixed environment will
   result in a broad area of at least isolated severe-wind potential.

   For parts of western/central Montana, convection that develops by
   early/mid afternoon near the high terrain is expected to spread
   eastward. This convection may be aided by a low-amplitude mid-level
   vort max moving around the periphery of the upper ridge. Increasing
   storm coverage and gradually strengthening mid/upper-level flow may
   allow for one or more outflow-driven clusters to develop and spread
   east-northeastward into early evening, with a threat of severe wind
   gusts. Localized gusts of 75+ mph cannot be ruled out, especially if
   deeper convection is able to spread into areas where very deep
   mixing and heating to near/above 100 F occurs prior to storm
   arrival. 

   Farther east, very strong heating is expected across parts of
   western/central South Dakota, with temperatures in some areas
   potentially rising to near/above 105 F. Low-level moisture will be
   relatively sparse, but MLCAPE of near/above 1000 J/kg will be
   possible where 50s F dewpoints can be maintained within the
   well-mixed environment. Initial storm development near the Black
   Hills will quickly become outflow dominant as it spreads eastward,
   with an increasing threat of severe gusts as storms tend to increase
   and potentially modestly organize as outflows aggregate. Some
   guidance depicts development of a persistent cluster or loosely
   organized MCS that moves eastward across South Dakota along a weak
   surface boundary. Should this occur, a corridor of more organized
   severe-wind threat (with gusts potentially near/above 75 mph) will
   be possible this evening.

   ...Upper Midwest/Upper Great Lakes...
   Conditional severe potential exists across parts of the Upper
   Midwest spanning Minnesota to Upper Michigan, although confidence
   regarding storm coverage and timing remains very low within the
   weakly forced environment characterized by generally rising upper
   heights. As morning stratus thins/erodes, moderate to strong
   destabilization is possible along/south of a surface front
   that is forecast to extend from northeast North Dakota into northern
   Minnesota/Wisconsin by late afternoon.

   Impressively long hodographs with effective shear of 50+ kt will be
   more than sufficient for storm organization, but most guidance
   remains rather muted regarding potential for diurnal storm
   development. If storms are able to develop during the afternoon or
   evening, then a threat for hail, damaging wind, and possibly a
   tornado could evolve. Even if diurnal storm development is limited,
   at least isolated vigorous elevated storms may evolve within a
   warm-advection regime near/north of the front late tonight, which
   would pose a threat for hail and perhaps locally damaging wind.

   ...Northeast Nevada/northern Utah/eastern Idaho...
   Storm development is expected during the afternoon across parts of
   northeast Nevada, along the western periphery of the upper ridge.
   These storms will spread into adjacent parts of southeast Idaho and
   northwest Utah with time. Strong to locally severe wind gusts will
   be possible with these storms as they move northeastward within a
   well-mixed environment.  

   ...South-central/southeast Arizona...
   High-based convection is again expected across southeast Arizona
   during the afternoon, with initial downburst potential. A modest
   enhancement to easterly flow along the southern periphery of the
   upper ridge may allow for localized strong to severe wind gusts to
   spread westward into parts of south-central Arizona, before
   convection weakens tonight.
