   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms with damaging wind gusts will be possible from this
   afternoon into early evening over parts of the Ohio Valley and
   Mid-Atlantic. Large hail and isolated severe wind gusts will also be
   possible in parts of the central High Plains.

   ...Lower MO Valley through the OH Valley into the Mid-Atlantic...
   Satellite and regional radar imagery shows several vorticity maxima
   embedded within the moderate westerly flow aloft that extends from
   the central Plains across the OH Valley and into the Mid-Atlantic.
   The strongest vorticity maximum is currently moving through
   north-central KS and south-central NE, while more subtle maxima
   exist across western IL, southwestern Lower MI/far northern IN, and
   western PA. Each of these features will likely contribute to showers
   and thunderstorms later today. The most consequential of these
   storms will likely occur in tandem with the western IL and
   southwestern Lower MI waves as they interact with a moist and
   diurnally destabilized airmass from the middle OH Valley into the
   Mid-Atlantic. Airmass destabilization appears likely by the early
   afternoon, with renewed thunderstorm development anticipated from
   southeast Lower MI into southern IN. Moderate vertical shear will be
   in place, supporting a predominantly multicellular mode. Mid-level
   winds may increase slightly throughout the afternoon, and a
   supercell or two is possible over southern OH. A trend towards
   clustering and the development of linear segments is anticipated
   over time, with damaging gusts as the primary risk throughout the
   day.

   These storms will continue eastward across WV into Mid-Atlantic
   during the late afternoon/early evening. Moderate buoyancy will
   support storm persistence and the potential for continued damaging
   gusts. Additional thunderstorm development is possible ahead of
   these clusters in areas of low-level convergence during the
   afternoon. Moderate vertical shear could support a few more
   organized storms, with water-loaded downbursts as the primary severe
   risk. A tornado or two is also possible, particularly across
   northern VA where southerly surface winds could result in adequate
   low-level hodograph curvature. 

   Farther west (across the Lower MO Valley), the well-defined
   vorticity maximum currently over north-central KS/southern-central
   NE is forecast to be east of the region by the late afternoon, but
   airmass destabilization in its wake is uncertain. If the airmass can
   recover, additional thunderstorms are likely along a cold front
   moving into the region this evening. Strong buoyancy will support
   robust updrafts, but modest vertical shear will keep storm
   organization limited. If thunderstorms form, a few strong gusts are
   possible across this region this evening.

   ...Northern/Central High Plains...
   Scattered thunderstorm development in anticipated this afternoon
   across the northern and central High Plains. Thunderstorms over the
   northern High Plains will be supported by low-level convergence
   along the western edge of the moist airmass over the Plains.
   Moderate vertical shear could support a few supercells capable of
   hail and strong gusts, but modest buoyancy should keep the overall
   severe threat isolated. 

   Thunderstorm development farther south across the central High
   Plains will result from a combination of moist low-level easterlies,
   westerly flow aloft and orographic effects. Here, more cellular
   development over the high terrain and immediate foothills could pose
   a risk for large hail before transitioning towards more linear
   structures with strong gusts as the primary severe risk.
