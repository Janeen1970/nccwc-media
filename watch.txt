   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms with severe wind gusts and hail will be possible this
   afternoon and evening in the northern Plains. Severe wind gusts will
   also be possible across southern Georgia and northern Florida, as
   well as the Great Basin/northern Intermountain West.

   ...20Z Update...
   The forecast appears to be on track, and minimal changes were needed
   with this update. Southward-spreading storm clusters in southern
   GA/northern FL will continue to pose a risk of damaging wind gusts
   through the afternoon into this evening. Any areas of upscale growth
   will pose the greatest severe-wind risk prior to the onset of
   nocturnal boundary-layer cooling. See Severe Thunderstorm Watch #532
   for more information. 

   Farther west over the northern Plains, a persistent cluster of
   thunderstorms and related cloud coverage over eastern MT/western ND
   cast uncertainty on the overall severe risk. However, as a
   subtle/low-amplitude midlevel wave currently over central MT
   overspreads the area, the ongoing activity and additional storm
   development along differential heating zones could pose an
   increasing risk of severe wind gusts and isolated hail into this
   evening. See MCD #1797 for more details.

   Additional thunderstorms forming along the high terrain from CO
   northward into eastern WY may also pose some risk of strong-severe
   wind gusts as storms move eastward into a warm/moist air mass over
   the High Plains. However, the severe-wind risk will be largely
   dependent on the development of robust cold pools and upscale
   growth, and weak forcing for ascent limits confidence in where this
   risk will be maximized.

   ..Weinman.. 07/29/2026

   .PREV DISCUSSION... /ISSUED 1101 AM CDT Wed Jul 29 2026/

   ...GA/FL...
   Latest surface analysis shows a weak cold front sagging southward
   across GA.  A very moist and unstable air mass is present along and
   south of the front, with dewpoints in the mid 70s and full heating
   expected.  This will yield afternoon MLCAPE values over 3000 J/kg
   and scattered thunderstorm development.  Sufficient northwesterly
   mid-level flow of 15-25 knots will help storms propagate into north
   FL by early evening.  Locally damaging wind gusts will be possible
   in the stronger cells due to water loading and some momentum
   transfer.  If outflows can congeal, a more organized damaging wind
   risk could develop late this afternoon and early evening before
   weakening after dark.

   ...MT/ND...
   Scattered thunderstorms are expected to develop this afternoon along
   a weak surface boundary near the MT/ND border.  These storms will be
   in a hot and moderately unstable air mass with MLCAPE around 2000
   J/kg.  As this activity spreads northeastward across western ND, the
   combination of a deep sub-cloud layer, steep low-level lapse rates,
   and sufficient flow aloft will pose the risk of damaging wind gusts.
