 Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1250 AM CDT Tue Sep 22 2026

   Valid 221200Z - 231200Z

   ...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PARTS OF
   THE SOUTHEAST...AND IN THE VICINITY OF NORTH-CENTRAL AND
   NORTHEASTERN UTAH....

   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms with isolated severe wind gusts are possible today
   across parts of the Southeast, and in the vicinity of north-central
   and northeastern Utah.

   ...Southeast...
   At the surface, a cold front will advance southward into the
   Southeast today. The position of the front by afternoon will be from
   the northern Gulf Coast States into southern Appalachians. Warming
   surface temperatures and increasing low-level convergence near and
   ahead of the boundary will result in scattered thunderstorm
   development this afternoon. Surface dewpoints across the moist
   airmass will mostly range from the mid 60s to mid 70s F. This will
   aid destabilization, with MLCAPE expected to peak in the 1500 to
   2500 J/kg in most areas ahead of the front. Although deep-layer
   shear will be limited, the thermodynamic environment will be able to
   support isolated severe wind gusts with convection that develops
   within the strongest localized instability.
    
   ...North-central and Northeastern Utah/Far Southwest Wyoming...
   A mid-level trough and low pressure center will move northeastward
   across the Intermountain West today. Ahead of the system, a surface
   low will deepen across western Utah. To the east and northeast of
   the low, surface dewpoints will range from the mid 40s to mid 50s F.
   This will be enough for the development of weak instability by
   afternoon. MLCAPE is forecast to increase to around 1000 J/kg over
   much of north-central and northeast Utah, as a cold pocket
   associated with the approaching upper-level low begins to affect the
   area. The Salt Lake City RAP forecast sounding at 21Z has 0-6 km
   shear near 40 knots with steep low to mid-level lapse rates in the 7
   to 8 C/km range. This should support an isolated potential for
   supercells. Hail and isolated severe gusts would be the primary
   threats.
