  
   Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   0738 AM CDT Thu Aug 27 2026

   Valid 271300Z - 281200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PARTS OF
   THE MID-ATLANTIC/NORTHEAST...AND THE CENTRAL PLAINS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Scattered strong to severe thunderstorms are possible across parts
   of the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic today. Damaging winds, severe
   hail, and a tornado or two may accompany these thunderstorms. Large
   to very large hail and severe gusts are also possible across parts
   of southwest South Dakota into western/central Nebraska and
   northwest Kansas.

   ...Mid-Atlantic/Northeast...
   An elongated mid/upper-level trough with closed low over Hudson Bay
   will encompass much of the eastern CONUS today. Embedded within this
   large-scale troughing, a mid-level shortwave trough with modestly
   enhanced southwesterly winds (around 25-35 kt) will advance
   northeastward through the day across the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast.
   At the surface, a weak low over eastern Ontario will develop into
   Quebec this afternoon, with a trailing cold front advancing
   southeastward across the Mid-Atlantic/Northeast through the period.
   Stronger daytime heating is forecast with southward extent across
   the Mid-Atlantic ahead of the cold front, with cloud cover
   potentially limiting heating somewhat across the Northeast. Even so,
   a zone of moderate instability should develop by this afternoon
   across these regions, with sufficient deep-layer shear for some
   updraft organization. Current expectations are for thunderstorms to
   gradually increase in coverage and intensity by 17-20Z ahead of the
   surface front and a lee trough along/east of the Appalachians. A mix
   of multicells and supercells should pose a threat for both severe
   hail and damaging winds, especially from the northern Mid-Atlantic
   northward into NY and vicinity. A tornado or two may also occur,
   with modest hodograph curvature noted in various NAM/RAP forecast
   soundings from northern MD northeastward to western New England.

   ...Central Plains...
   The central Plains will be sandwiched between upper ridging over the
   Southwest and upper troughing over eastern Canada today, with modest
   northwesterly flow aloft persisting through the period. Large-scale
   ascent is forecast to remain rather weak/nebulous, with some
   guidance suggesting a weak mid-level impulse will crest the ridge
   and aid convective initiation across parts of southwest SD into
   western/central NE this afternoon. A corridor of greater low-level
   moisture (generally 60s surface dewpoints) will be in place across
   this region. Daytime heating of this moist airmass coupled with the
   presence of steep mid-level lapse rates will help foster moderate to
   locally strong instability and erode lingering MLCIN by 19-21Z.
   While robust convective development and overall coverage still
   remains somewhat uncertain given the weak forcing, a rather
   favorable environment for supercells will exist. Any sustained
   supercells will be capable of producing large hail (some 2+ inches)
   as they track south-southeastward through the late afternoon into
   the evening. The potential for severe/damaging gusts is also
   uncertain, as most guidance does not show a cluster developing this
   evening. An isolated threat for severe hail/gusts may also exist
   farther west/south across the CO Front Range to Raton Mesa vicinity,
   although deep-layer shear should be weaker across these areas.

   ...Northern Rockies/High Plains...
   A mid-level jet on the southeast periphery of upper troughing over
   the eastern Pacific and western Canada will translate northeastward
   across the northern Rockies today. Enhanced west-southwesterly flow
   and lift associated with this jet will likely aid isolated to
   scattered thunderstorm development this afternoon across parts of
   the northern Rockies, with this activity spreading into the adjacent
   High Plains through the evening. While low-level moisture will
   remain limited/shallow across these regions, steep low/mid-level
   lapse rates amid a well-mixed boundary layer coupled with moderate
   to strong deep-layer shear should support a threat for isolated
   severe gusts with the high-based convection that develops.

   ...Great Basin...
   High-based thunderstorms should also develop this afternoon across
   portions of northeast NV into northwest UT and southeast ID. While
   low/mid-level winds and related thunderstorm organization will
   likely be somewhat weaker/limited compared to areas farther north,
   steep lapse rates and large DCAPE will support potential for strong
   to severe outflow gusts as this convection spreads northeastward
   into the early evening.

   ...South-Central/Coastal Texas into Southern Louisiana...
   Ongoing thunderstorms across southern LA may pose a threat for gusty
   winds this morning before they move offshore. Additional
   thunderstorms may develop this afternoon from parts of south-central
   TX towards the upper TX Coast, with isolated severe/damaging
   downburst winds possible given ample moisture/instability forecast.
   Weak deep-layer shear should limit the overall severe threat.
