   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS FOR NORTHEAST

   CORRECTED FOR WIND LINES

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated to widely scattered severe thunderstorms capable primarily
   of a severe-wind threat will be possible this afternoon and evening
   over parts of Ozarks and Great Plains.  Isolated strong to locally
   severe gusts capable of wind damage are possible from the
   Mid-Atlantic northward into New York.

   ...Ozarks westward into the southern High Plains...
   Water-vapor imagery shows a mid-level anticyclone centered over
   AZ/UT and a broad trough over the Upper Midwest favoring modest
   west-northwesterly mid to high-level flow across the central Plains.
   Several clusters of showers/thunderstorms are ongoing this morning
   from the mid MS Valley and lower MO Valley into the OK-KS border
   region.  Much of this activity is located to the north of a residual
   front and outflow draped from the TX Panhandle east-northeast into
   the mid MS Valley.  South of the boundary, an appreciably moist
   airmass, featuring 50s F dewpoints west to lower 70s east, will heat
   in areas void of convection and related cloud debris.  It is
   uncertain how much influence morning showers/storms will have across
   the Slight Risk area.  Nonetheless, model forecast soundings show
   moderate destabilization occurring by mid-late afternoon across much
   of this region.  Isolated to widely scattered storms will
   preferentially develop near the effective front/outflow.  Some storm
   organization is possible with the main threat being isolated to
   widely scattered severe gusts.  Have adjusted the level 1 intensity
   for wind to northern OK westward into the southern High Plains where
   steep low-level lapse rates are forecast.  A couple of multicell
   clusters may eventually evolve during the early evening.  A relative
   minimum in storm activity may occur over parts of the TX Panhandle
   in between storms over the northeast NM/western part of the TX
   Panhandle and higher coverage of storms farther east over northern
   OK into the Ozarks.  

   ...Mid-Atlantic into the Northeast...
   Mid-level flow will be southwesterly over the Northeast today.  At
   the surface, a trough will be located from the Atlantic Coastal
   Plains of Virginia north-northeastward into the southeastern New
   York.  As surface temperatures warm today, thunderstorms will
   develop in the foothills of the Appalachians, moving
   east-northeastward into the lower elevations and toward the surface
   trough.  Surface dewpoints near the trough will be in the lower to
   mid 70s F, which will contribute to moderate destabilization by
   early afternoon (MLCAPE 1000-2500 J/kg).  Some of the stronger
   water-loaded downdrafts will be capable of an isolated risk for wind
   damage.  

   ...Western Nebraska/southern South Dakota...
   West-northwesterly mid-level flow will be in place over much of the
   north-central U.S.  At the surface, an axis of low-level
   moisture will set up from western Nebraska into far southern South
   Dakota, where dewpoints will be in the 60s F.  Near this axis, model
   forecasts show a pocket of moderate instability this afternoon with
   MLCAPE around 2000 J/kg.  Although large-scale ascent will be weak,
   any cell that can initiate near the instability axis could be
   capable of producing isolated severe gusts/large hail.
