Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   0726 AM CDT Wed Sep 23 2026

   Valid 231300Z - 241200Z

   ...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS FOR PARTS OF THE
   WESTERN DAKOTAS AND SOUTHERN APPALACHIANS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated severe thunderstorms are possible this afternoon across
   parts of the western Dakotas and southern Appalachians.

   ...Western Dakotas...
   Water-vapor imagery this morning shows a mid-level trough moving
   northeast across WY/MT.  This upper disturbance will move into the
   western Dakotas by late afternoon and gradually deamplify as it
   reaches the Red River Valley by daybreak Thursday.  A lee trough
   will facilitate the maintenance of a moisture plume extending
   northward from the central High Plains into the western Dakotas. 
   Model forecast soundings show around 1000 J/kg MLCAPE developing
   with marginally steep lapse rates.  Widely scattered thunderstorms
   are forecast to develop as convective inhibition erodes in the
   vicinity of the lee trough and the Black Hills this afternoon.  Some
   elongation of the hodograph may aid in some storm organization
   potential, with the stronger thunderstorms perhaps yielding an
   isolated risk for marginally severe hail/gusts through early
   evening.  

   ...Southern Appalachians...
   A mid-level vorticity maximum will move southeast today from the
   lower OH Valley into the southern Appalachians.  An inverted trough
   co-located with a regionally greater reservoir of low-level moisture
   (i.e., surface dewpoints in the 60s deg F), will focus thunderstorm
   development this afternoon.  Extensive low cloud cover will likely
   inhibit strong heating and lends considerable uncertainty regarding
   destabilization and the potential for stronger thunderstorm
   development.  Forecast soundings show that despite limited low to
   mid-level lapse rates, perhaps 250-750 J/kg MLCAPE may develop by
   mid afternoon.  Strong to locally damaging thunderstorm gusts may
   occur with the stronger storms.  Have removed low hail probabilities
   due in part to the 12 UTC Nashville, TN raob depicting poor 700-500
   mb lapse rates (4.8 deg C/km) and limited forecast buoyancy.
