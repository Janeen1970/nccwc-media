 Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1250 AM CDT Wed Jul 22 2026

   Valid 221200Z - 231200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS THE
   SOUTHERN MID-ATLANTIC...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated to scattered storms are possible in the southern
   Mid-Atlantic into New England this afternoon/evening. Damaging winds
   are the primary concern. Isolated strong to severe wind gusts are
   also possible in the central High Plains and central Oregon.

   ...Synopsis...
   A upper trough will persist in the Northeast CONUS with a ridge
   amplifying into the Northwest. An offshore upper low in the
   Northwest will eject shortwave perturbations into Oregon/Washington.
   At the surface, a cold front will move through New England. The
   front will slow in the Mid-Atlantic during the afternoon before
   moving southeast during the overnight.

   ...Mid-Atlantic into parts of Carolinas...
   The cold front in this region is expected to slow/stall near the
   Blue Ridge into eastern Maryland. Models suggest
   clouds/precipitation will be likely in the vicinity of the
   Virginia/North Carolina border. Mid-level forcing for ascent will be
   weak at best and subtle mid-level warming is expected from far
   southern Virginia into the Carolinas. Moderate to strong mid-level
   winds will be in place with the strongest flow in Virginia/Maryland.
   It is not clear how convection will evolve during the day/afternoon.
   Storms could potentially continue from the morning into the
   afternoon over portions of the region. There also is potential for
   storms to develop along the cold front where there could potentially
   be greater surface heating. In the Carolinas, the lack of ascent and
   cloud cover concerns limit confidence in how many storms and how
   strong they will become. Greater confidence in storm coverage exists
   in Virginia into the DelMarVa where the Slight Risk has been
   maintained. The kinematic environment will support occasionally
   organized storms capable of damaging winds and perhaps a tornado or
   two.

   ...New England...
   A strong mid-level jet will be favorably timed during the afternoon,
   particularly for New Hampshire/Maine. As the cold front  moves
   through, scattered storms appear probable. Effective shear of 30-40
   kt and strong upper-level winds will promote supercell structures.
   The shear vector will have a large component parallel to the
   north-south cold front so it is not clear how long discrete
   structures can be maintained. Isolated damaging winds, marginally
   severe hail, and perhaps a tornado would be possible. Some
   consideration was given to a higher severe probabilities, but the
   region will initially be in the wake of a shortwave trough and
   thermodynamic profiles exhibit characteristics (e.g., mid-level
   inversion, lowered equilibrium level) that limits confidence in how
   many severe-caliber storms will exist.

   Farther south and west, there is less signal for convective
   development along the cold front. A Marginal risk has been
   maintained this outlook cycle. Some risk for damaging winds would
   exist if an isolated storm can develop along the front.

   ...Central High Plains...
   Southeasterly surface winds will combine with increasingly
   westerly/northwesterly flow aloft to promote modest deep-layer shear
   (around 30 kt 0-6 km) during the afternoon/evening. The development
   of a weak lee low and low-level moisture moving northwest around the
   Midwest anticyclone will aid in storm development within the region.
   Temperature-dewpoint spreads at the surface will allow for efficient
   outflow production. A strengthening southeasterly low-level jet in
   western Kansas may also help maintain convection farther east during
   the evening. Severe wind gusts are possible with this activity.

   ...Oregon...
   An upper-low will be positioned off the Northwest Coast. Subtle
   ascent will help to initiate storms in parts of central Oregon
   during the afternoon. Models are in strong agreement that scattered
   storms will move northward into at least early evening. Strong
   heating will lead to large temperature-dewpoint spreads at the
   surface. Inverted-v thermodynamic profiles will promote a risk for
   strong to severe wind gusts despite limited buoyancy and shear.
