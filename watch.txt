 Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1153 PM CDT Fri Aug 07 2026

   Valid 081200Z - 091200Z

   ...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PORTIONS
   OF THE NORTHERN AND CENTRAL PLAINS INTO THE UPPER MISSISSIPPI
   VALLEY...PARTS OF THE MID-ATLANTIC...AND SOUTHERN ARIZONA...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Severe thunderstorms will be possible this afternoon and evening
   across parts of the central and northern Plains southeastward into
   the lower to mid Missouri Valley. Additional strong to severe storms
   are possible in the Mid-Atlantic and central/southern Arizona.

   ...Northern/Central Plains into MN/IA/MO...

   A modest but weakening low-level jet across portions of NE/SD may
   support ongoing elevated convection this morning, mainly across NE.
   This activity will weaken and spread southeast through the morning
   hours, but could pose a marginal risk for hail. 

   The forecast for the afternoon into nighttime hours becomes complex
   and uncertain. Broad northwesterly flow aloft will modestly
   strengthen by late afternoon/early evening as several shortwave
   impulses migrate through the base of an upper low/trough centered
   over central Canada. Height falls are expected to be weak, and warm
   mid-level temperatures will pose capping issues. Nevertheless,
   surface troughing across the northern/central High Plains will
   result in south/southeasterly low-level flow, transporting 60s F
   dewpoints northward across the central Plains and MO Valley ahead of
   a southward sagging surface front oriented across ND and northern MN
   late in the period. Increasing boundary layer moisture beneath steep
   midlevel lapse rates will support moderate to strong
   destabilization. 

   Several areas of convection may develop during the late
   afternoon/evening near the surface trough, and overnight near the
   southward-sagging surface front. Given weak large-scale ascent and
   warm midlevels, it remains uncertain where storm development is most
   likely and how storm coverage will evolve. With an increasing
   low-level jet across the central Plains during the nighttime hours,
   some convection could develop atop a capping inversion, or along the
   surface front near the ND/SD border. Isolated large hail is possible
   with this activity. If surface-based convection can develop within
   the strongly unstable airmass, damaging wind gusts also will be
   possible.  

   Additional high-based convection is possible across CO and western
   KS/NE. A hot and well mixed boundary layer could support strong
   outflow winds with this activity as well.

   ...Mid-Atlantic...

   A modest midlevel shortwave trough will overspread the region
   through afternoon/early evening. Strong heating of a seasonally
   moist airmass will support moderate instability. Vertical shear is
   expected to be sufficient for organized cells and clusters with an
   attendant risk for damaging wind gusts. The strongest cells could
   also produce small hail. 

   ...AZ...

   Easterly midlevel flow will persist on the southern periphery of an
   upper high over the Four Corners. Steep midlevel lapse rates and
   persistent moisture across the region will foster thunderstorm
   development across the higher terrain during the evening. The hot
   and well mixed boundary layer will support strong outflow gusts as
   convection moves westward off higher terrain toward the lower
   deserts.
