   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated to widely scattered strong to severe thunderstorms remain
   possible tonight across parts of the central and southern High
   Plains, parts of Arkansas into the lower Mississippi Valley, and
   parts of the Midwest and Ohio Valley.

   ...Central/southern High Plains...
   Widely scattered strong to severe storms are ongoing this evening
   across parts of the central/southern High Plains. The most favorable
   environment resides from eastern CO into western KS, with moderate
   buoyancy and effective shear of 30-40 kt within a northwesterly flow
   regime. The strongest discrete cells in this area will continue to
   pose a threat of hail and localized severe gusts. Some modest
   clustering remains possible with time, which could result in at
   least an isolated severe-wind threat as convection spreads into
   western KS later tonight. 

   Deep-layer flow/shear is generally weaker across the southern High
   Plains, but localized strong to severe gusts will remain possible
   within the well-mixed environment through at least dusk. 
    
   ...AR into the Lower MS Valley...
   Widespread deep convection from southeastern OK into
   central/southern AR may eventually evolve into a loosely organized,
   outflow-driven cluster with time this evening. This could result in
   a threat for scattered wind damage spreading into northern LA
   through the evening, with an isolated threat possible later tonight
   as storms approach the LA/MS Gulf Coasts. 

   ...Midwest/Ohio Valley...
   Scattered storms are ongoing from parts of Lower MI into parts of
   the Midwest/Ohio Valley. The combination of moderate buoyancy and
   marginal deep-layer shear (generally 25-35 kt of effective shear)
   has only been supportive of an isolated severe threat thus far. No
   substantial improvement in the environment is expected through the
   evening, but ongoing storms may continue to pose an isolated severe
   threat before convection generally subsides later tonight.
