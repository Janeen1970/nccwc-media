   0744 PM CDT Sat Aug 01 2026

   Valid 020100Z - 021200Z

   ...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS THIS EVENING
   ACROSS PARTS OF THE OHIO AND TENNESSEE VALLEYS AND PARTS OF THE
   SOUTHERN GREAT PLAINS/ROCKIES...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Thunderstorms posing a risk for generally brief, weak tornadoes
   across the southwestern Ohio vicinity probably will begin to
   diminish within the next hour or two.  Otherwise, locally strong to
   severe gusts remain possible this evening in continuing thunderstorm
   development across parts of the Ohio and Tennessee Valleys, and
   parts of the southern Rockies into adjacent southern Great Plains.

   ...01Z Update...

   ...Ohio/Tennessee Valleys...
   Focused low-level convergence and weak to modest clockwise-curved
   low-level hodographs, near a surface low now centered to the
   northwest of the Greater Cincinnati area, have contributed to
   transient supercell structures within developing convection
   downstream of an elongating mid-level low across the lower
   Ohio/middle Mississippi Valley vicinity.  Potential for brief/weak
   tornadoes has probably been boosted by near-surface buoyancy
   associated with a boundary layer characterized by high relative
   humidity and surface dew points in the lower 70s, along at least a
   narrow pre-frontal corridor near the southern Ohio/Indiana state
   border area into north central Kentucky.  However, with the loss of
   daytime heating, near-surface stabilization probably will diminish
   this threat by 02-03Z.

   Otherwise, some intensification of pre-frontal thunderstorm
   development still appears possible this evening, southward across
   parts of middle and eastern Tennessee into northern Alabama. 
   Although lower/mid-tropospheric lapse rates are rather modest to
   weak, seasonably high moisture content is supporting sizable CAPE. 
   Aided by 20-30 kt deep-layer southwesterly mean flow overspreading
   this environment, heavy precipitation loading and downward momentum
   transfer could still contribute to potential for localized damaging
   surface gusts.

   ...Southern Rockies and adjacent southern Great Plains...
   Convection developing southward ahead of a weak perturbation within
   light northerly flow, into a very warm and deeply mixed boundary
   layer with 40-50 F surface temperature/dew point spreads, may
   continue to pose a risk for strong to locally severe wind gusts into
   mid/late evening across parts of southern New Mexico and adjacent
   portions of western/southwestern Texas and southeastern Arizona.
