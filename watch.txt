   Isolated severe thunderstorm wind gusts and possibly a tornado or
   two could occur this afternoon into tonight from southeast Arizona
   across southern and eastern New Mexico into west Texas.

   ...20z Update...
   No changes were made to the existing severe probabilities with this
   update as the forecast remains generally on track. An increase in
   thunderstorm coverage (likely in the form of a complex/linear storm
   mode) is expected this afternoon/evening as the remnant core of Polo
   progresses northeastward into the southern High Plains. As this
   occurs, isolated severe wind gusts and perhaps a tornado or two may
   occur, with locally greater potential possible from southeastern New
   Mexico into West Texas where less prevalent cloud cover has allowed
   for stronger diurnal heating through mid afternoon. See the
   discussion below for additional details.

   ..Chalmers/Thornton.. 09/29/2026

   .PREV DISCUSSION... /ISSUED 1120 AM CDT Tue Sep 29 2026/

   ...Synopsis...
   Fairly complex synoptic evolution is anticipated today through
   tonight as a remnants of what is currently Hurricane Polo become
   absorbed within the circulation of the deepening mid-latitude
   cyclone currently centered over the Lower CO River Valley. In
   general, the Lower CO River Valley cyclone is forecast to deepen as
   it gradually shifts eastward. In response, Hurricane Polo is
   forecast to move quickly northeastward across Sonora and into New
   Mexico. Polo is forecast to weaken over the mountainous terrain of
   northern Mexico, but will likely maintain some of its warm-core
   characteristics through at least tomorrow morning. Additionally,
   mass response across the southern High Plains will result in
   increasing low-level moisture advection across the southern Plains
   while also aiding in surface cyclogenesis across central and eastern
   NM late tonight/early tomorrow.

   ...Southern Arizona... 
   Recent surface analysis reveals a very moist airmass over southern
   AZ, with a corridor of mid to upper 60s dewpoints extending from the
   central southern border into the Phoenix vicinity. Surface analysis
   also places a weak low over south-central AZ. VAD profiles from IWA
   and EMX both show fairly long hodographs, supported by 30 to 40 kt
   of low/mid-level southwesterly flow. Widespread showers and
   thunderstorms will likely continue throughout the day, with limited
   diurnal heating and weak tropical lapse rates keeping buoyancy
   modest. Even so, the strong nature of the wind profiles could
   support a few at least weak/transient supercells, and potentially
   more organized convective bands later today, with at least some
   potential for strong thunderstorm wind gusts.

   ...Southern High Plains...
   Thunderstorm coverage is expected to increase notably this
   afternoon/evening as the core of Polo progresses into the region.
   Tropical character of the airmass will keep lapse rates poor while
   cloud cover also limits heating, limiting the overall buoyancy.
   Nonetheless, buoyant thermodynamic profiles are still expected to
   result in updrafts deep enough for lightning production.
   Additionally, low-level wind profiles are expected to lengthen
   significantly after 00Z as the remnants of Polo interact with a
   dynamically strengthening low-level jet. The widespread storm
   coverage will result in a tendency toward complex or linear storm
   modes with time, though a few/episodic supercells will also be
   possible. A brief tornado and/or strong wind gusts could accompany
   any organized, more persistent storm structures.
