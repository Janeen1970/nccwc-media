   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated severe storms will be possible mainly later this afternoon
   into tonight across the Upper Midwest/upper Great Lakes, the central
   Plains, and across the lower Colorado River Valley and Great Basin.

   ...Great Lakes Region...
   Water vapor imagery shows a subtle shortwave trough moving across
   NE.  This feature is forecast to track northeastward through the day
   and affect the Great Lakes region by late afternoon. Relatively
   strong heating is expected across much of WI, along with dewpoints
   in the upper 60s to lower 70s.  This along with moderately steep
   mid-level lapse rates will yield afternoon MLCAPE values over 2500
   J/kg.  A cold front will sag into the region by early evening,
   providing the focus for widely scattered thunderstorms from central
   WI into upper MI.  This activity will track east-southeastward
   across WI and into lower MI.  Forecast soundings suggest sufficient
   vertical shear to support rotating storms capable of locally
   gusty/damaging winds and perhaps some hail.  

   ...Eastern CO into NE...
   A persistent plume of mid-level moisture continues to affect areas
   from AZ into CO, with another round of afternoon thunderstorms
   expected over the mountains.  As these storms spread eastward into
   eastern CO, strong outflow winds may accompany the more intense
   cores.  Isolated strong storms may persist into parts of northwest
   KS and much of NE after dark.

   ...UT/AZ...
   A shortwave trough over CA will track eastward and provide
   large-scale forcing for ascent today over parts of AZ/UT.  This will
   result in scattered thunderstorms once again this afternoon across
   much of UT and northern AZ.  If pockets of sufficient heating can
   develop, locally damaging wind gusts could occur.
