   .PREV DISCUSSION... /ISSUED 1127 AM CDT Fri Sep 11 2026/

   ...Upper Midwest/central Plains...
   An eastward-progressive belt of quasi-zonal westerlies exists over
   the northern half of the CONUS, to the south of upper troughing over
   the Canadian Prairies/central Canada, and the east of the amplifying
   upper trough off the coastal Pacific Northwest. Weak frontal wave
   development will occur from eastern South Dakota into Minnesota as a
   cold front spreads eastward, with the front eventually overtaking a
   pre-frontal trough/dryline across the central Plains.

   A narrow corridor of 60s F surface dewpoints are in place ahead of
   the cold front/dryline, with diurnal heating aiding moderate
   destabilization along with steepened mid-level lapse rates. Minimal
   MLCIN is expected along/ahead of the front in far northeast South
   Dakota/western Minnesota into/just after peaking heating. Convective
   development appears likely in this time frame across this region,
   especially across Minnesota, owing to sufficient low-level
   convergence along the front coupled with forcing for ascent
   associated with the mid-level shortwave trough.

   Strongly veering and gradually strengthening winds with height
   through low/mid-levels, and 35-45 kt of deep-layer shear will easily
   support organized convection. Initial supercells should pose a
   threat for large hail (isolated 2+ inches possible), but convective
   mode will probably trend towards clustering fairly quickly given the
   presence of the cold front and strengthening low-level jet this
   evening. Any supercells that can remain at least semi-discrete into
   the early evening may also have a tornado threat with greater
   low-level shear forecast in this time frame. Scattered
   severe/damaging winds should occur with any clusters or bowing line
   segments that can develop and spread eastward through the evening.
   However, the wind threat will likely wane with eastward extent
   across Minnesota into western Wisconsin by late this evening given
   the narrow zone of destabilization forecast.

   Farther southwest, convective initiation and overall thunderstorm
   coverage from southeast South Dakota/southwest Minnesota
   southwestward into Nebraska and northern Kansas are more uncertain
   and probably more limited/conditional late this afternoon and
   evening, as the better forcing for ascent with the shortwave trough
   will remain regionally focused to the north. Still, a favorable
   thermodynamic environment and sufficient (25-35 kt) deep-layer shear
   would conditionally support severe hail/wind with any thunderstorms
   that can develop and be sustained.

   ...Mid-South/Tennessee Valley/Carolinas and Virginia...
   Multiple convectively augmented mid-level perturbations remain
   evident at midday in satellite/radar imagery, including over the
   Ozarks as well as the middle Gulf Coast in vicinity of southern
   Alabama. A stalled surface front extending from the ArkLaTex
   east-northeastward into the Tennessee Valley and Virginia will
   provide a focus for thunderstorms. Generally speaking, expectations
   are for generally disorganized/pulse-type convection to pose a
   threat for occasional damaging winds as diurnal heating destabilizes
   the boundary layer and steepens low-level lapse rates. However, a
   modest enhancement to mid-level flow across parts of the Mid-South
   (25 kt mid-levels per Little Rock AR WSR-88D VWP), as well as
   Virginia, could promote a couple of loosely organized clusters with
   a bit more of a focused damaging wind threat.
