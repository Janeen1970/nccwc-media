   ...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS PARTS OF
   THE MIDWEST/OHIO VALLEY AND UTAH INTO FAR NORTHWEST COLORADO...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Isolated strong to severe thunderstorms appear possible today across
   parts of the Midwest/Ohio Valley and Utah/far northwest Colorado.

   ...Mid Mississippi Valley/Midwest into the Ohio Valley...
   In between upper troughing over Canada and upper ridging over the
   Southeast, a convectively augmented mid-level vorticity maximum over
   IL/northwest IN evident on radar imagery will track eastward today
   across the OH Valley/southern Great Lakes. While mid-level westerly
   winds will remain fairly modest across this region (generally 20-30
   kt or less), this feature should aid additional convective
   development this afternoon across parts of IN into OH and vicinity
   as a moist low-level airmass gradually destabilizes. Poor lapse
   rates aloft should tend to limit overall updraft strength, but
   locally damaging winds may occur with any loosely organized clusters
   that can form and spread eastward through the early evening before
   weakening with the loss of daytime heating.

   Most guidance continues to show thunderstorms developing late this
   evening (mainly after 17/03Z) over portions of IA/northern IL in a
   modest low-level warm advection regime. While MUCAPE will gradually
   increase across this region as low-level moisture streams northward,
   the potential for severe/damaging winds should tend to remain low
   since convection will remain elevated and well north of a stalled
   surface front. Cloud-layer shear also appears marginal to support
   supercells with hail potential. Given these limiting factors, have
   not included low severe probabilities at this time.

   ...Southwest into the Great Basin...
   On the southeast periphery of a closed upper low centered over
   northern CA, around 35-45 kt of southwesterly mid-level flow will
   persist today across parts of the Great Basin. Ongoing thunderstorms
   across eastern UT/northwest CO are occurring in a strongly sheared
   but weakly unstable environment. While occasional hail could occur
   with the stronger cores, current expectations are for renewed
   thunderstorm development later this afternoon as the rather moist
   low-level airmass gradually destabilizes with filtered diurnal
   heating. If any cells/small clusters can form, they would pose an
   isolated threat for severe hail/gusts. But, the limited
   heating/instability should temper the overall severe risk.

   Farther south across AZ/NM, numerous to widespread thunderstorms are
   expected to develop and spread northward through the period as a
   weak mid-level perturbation moves northward from the Baja California
   vicinity across the Southwest/Four Corners. Above-average low-level
   moisture (50s/60s surface dewpoints) is already in place over these
   areas, but modest lapse rates aloft and limited daytime heating due
   to widespread cloud cover suggest that instability will remain weak.
   Nearly unidirectional/southerly flow and modest strengthening with
   height through mid/upper levels should foster only marginal
   deep-layer shear to support updraft organization. While occasional
   gusty winds could occur with the stronger cores that develop, the
   threat for severe gusts appears too limited to include any
   probabilities with this update.
