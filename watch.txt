Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1238 AM CDT Tue Sep 08 2026

   Valid 081200Z - 091200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS OVER PORTIONS OF
   IOWA AND EASTERN NEBRASKA...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Widely scattered severe storms capable of mainly severe wind gusts
   appear possible across portions of Iowa and eastern Nebraska Tuesday
   afternoon and evening. More sporadic occurrences of severe winds and
   large hail may occur from portions of the upper Midwest into the
   central Plains.

   ...Synopsis...

   An intense upper low moving into southern SK late this evening will
   continue northeast through central MB into northern ON ahead of a
   secondary short-wave trough tracking from the northern Rockies into
   the northern Plains. A belt of enhanced mid/upper-level winds will
   be maintained between those features from the northern High Plains
   into the upper Great Lakes. At the surface, a cold front will
   advance through the eastern Dakotas and upper MS Valley with the
   trailing extension of the boundary moving more slowly southeast
   through the mid-MO Valley and central High Plains.


   ...Central Plains into the upper Midwest...

   Clusters of thunderstorms are likely to be ongoing at 12Z Tuesday
   from portions of the mid-MO Valley into the upper Midwest within a
   zone of warm advection and moisture transport along a nocturnal
   low-level jet (LLJ). Showers and storms may linger through the
   morning across parts of WI and the U.P. of MI, limiting the degree
   of air mass destabilization and associated severe weather risk
   during the afternoon. Farther to the southwest from IA through
   eastern and southern NE into northern KS, stronger daytime heating
   on the southern fringe of lingering mid/high-level cloudiness will
   contribute to a well-mixed boundary layer, featuring steep low-level
   lapse rates. When combined with boundary-layer dewpoints in the 60s
   to low/mid 70s F, the air mass is expected to become moderately to
   strongly unstable by late afternoon with MLCAPE of 2000-3000 J/kg. 

   In the absence of appreciable height falls/large-scale forcing for
   ascent, convergence along the front and any preceding confluence
   zones/outflow boundaries is expected to foster a gradual increase in
   storm coverage and intensity by mid to late afternoon amidst the
   destabilizing air mass. The strongest deep-layer shear is expected
   to be displaced to the north of the effective warm sector with
   around 30-35 kt forecast along the frontal zone. A largely parallel
   shear-vector orientation with the front suggests that initial storms
   should grow upscale into line segments or a broken band with an
   associated risk for severe wind gusts from mid afternoon through
   evening. Isolated occurrences of marginally severe hail are
   possible; however, poor mid-level lapse are expected to limit the
   overall threat. The 00Z models do indicate some LLJ enhancement over
   central IA by late afternoon into evening, resulting in a corridor
   of stronger low-level shear.  While a brief tornado or two are
   possible, a more robust threat is expected to be limited by largely
   outflow-dominant storms.
