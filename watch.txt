  Day 1 Convective Outlook  
   NWS Storm Prediction Center Norman OK
   1259 AM CDT Mon Aug 24 2026

   Valid 241200Z - 251200Z

   ...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF SEVERE THUNDERSTORMS ACROSS THE
   NORTHERN PLAINS/UPPER MIDWEST...CENTRAL HIGH PLAINS...MIDDLE GULF
   COAST AND COASTAL CAROLINAS...

   ...SUMMARY...
   Severe thunderstorms capable of large hail and damaging wind gusts
   will be possible mainly this afternoon and evening across the
   Dakotas and northwest Minnesota as well as the central High Plains.
   Other severe storms may focus across the lower Mississippi Valley
   and middle Gulf Coast, as well as the coastal Carolinas.

   ...Dakotas/Upper Midwest to central High Plains...
   An eastward-moving shortwave trough will emerge from the Canadian
   Rockies and move across the Canadian prairies through tonight, with
   a belt of moderately strong flow aloft and multiple MCVs influencing
   the Dakotas to Minnesota. Residual early day convection and
   lingering cloud cover will be complicating factors as far as the
   later-day forecast scenario. 

   That said, at least widely scattered thunderstorms should develop
   and increase by late afternoon within a moist/unstable air mass near
   the eastward-moving front and weak surface wave across the
   east-central Dakotas and/or cross the international border and move
   southeastward into the region. Deep-layer wind profiles would be
   conducive to some supercells and the development of
   east/southeastward-moving clusters by evening, with large hail and
   damaging winds possible.

   More removed from the stronger cyclonic flow aloft, additional
   higher-based storms are expected by late afternoon and early evening
   south-southwestward across the Black Hills/western Nebraska in
   vicinity of the front and preceding trough. Sufficient
   strength/veering of winds with height should support some initial
   supercells capable of hail, prior to a clustering of these
   southeastward-moving storms, with wind damage being a possibility
   through mid/late evening across the central High Plains.  

   ...Coastal Carolinas...
   The region will be influenced by moderately strong cyclonic flow
   aloft, with a moist and moderate-to-strongly unstable environment
   along the coastal plain to the east of a stationary/slow-moving
   front. The degree of buoyancy and upwards of 35-40 kt effective
   shear could support some initial supercells with hail this
   afternoon, although damaging winds should be the most prevalent
   concern as storms become more numerous. Severe potential should tend
   to wane by around sunset.

   ...Lower Mississippi Valley/Middle Gulf Coast...
   Widely scattered elevated convection has developed early in the
   overnight across the Ozarks, and this regime may increase and
   probably shift south-southeastward today, potentially becoming
   surface based into the afternoon, influenced by cold pool
   development. It seems plausible that an upscale-growing/forward
   propagating cluster could gradually evolve during the day, with
   damaging wind potential increasing across the ArkLaMiss
   south-southeastward toward the Gulf Coast.

   ...Northern Great Basin/central Rockies...
   Scattered high-based thunderstorms are expected to develop this
   afternoon amid classic inverted-V profiles. Since these storms will
   develop along the periphery of stronger flow aloft, some
   organization is possible. Multiple dry microbursts may occur, some
   of which may pose a severe gust threat.

   ...Maine...
   By late morning into early afternoon, partially clearing skies will
   encourage surface temperatures to rise into the 70s F, resulting in
   marginal instability (e.g. a few hundred J/kg MLCAPE) given
   relatively poor mid-level lapse rates. Nonetheless, a southerly
   mid-to upper-level jet streak will overspread northern New England
   during the afternoon, resulting in highly elongated hodographs and
   30-40 kt of effective shear. As such, any storms that can develop
   and become sustained should become organized multicells capable of
   supporting a few damaging gusts and possibly some hail.
